# Mycelium Purpose

Mycelium is an open-source academic research assistant that any lab can run on its own computers. It helps labs understand their own goals, people, projects, papers, funding needs, and opportunities as one connected research ecosystem.

The product is built around a simple idea: research groups do not only need another place to store papers or ask generic AI questions. They need a living profile of the lab that can connect literature, claims, evidence, methods, datasets, grants, fellowships, deadlines, skills, and member interests into practical guidance.

Underneath, Mycelium is a grounding layer: the paper and opportunity corpus, lab and member profiles, the knowledge graph, the opportunity feeds, and the evaluation harness that measures retrieval. Research tools are built on top of that layer and read the lab's context from it. The feeds, daily reports, reminders, and Mylo in Discord are the first of those tools; the next ones are listed in [`goals.md`](goals.md#tools-on-the-grounding-layer). A tool earns its place when it is better with the lab's context behind it and can be proven against a public benchmark.

Mycelium should help a lab answer questions like:

- What changed in our field this week?
- Which papers matter to our current projects?
- What claims are supported, challenged, or still uncertain?
- Which grants fit the lab's roadmap?
- Which fellowships fit specific students or trainees?
- What proposal, publication, or collaboration should we work on next?

The long-term vision is a living AI profile for a research lab and its members. That profile should continuously map research activity into evidence-backed reports, opportunity alerts, proposal readiness, student development support, collaboration suggestions, and strategic action plans.

Mycelium should be useful because it is specific to the lab. It should not feel like a generic search tool or chatbot. It should behave like a calm research operations partner that understands the lab's direction, remembers what matters, explains why recommendations fit, and keeps people oriented as research, funding, and team needs change.

The project should balance intelligence with trust. Every recommendation should be explainable, permission-aware, and grounded in sources or known lab context. The system should make research work easier without hiding uncertainty, eligibility limits, privacy concerns, or the need for human review.
