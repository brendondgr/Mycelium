# EXP-2026-09-011 — Results

## 1. Headline

Postgres answers all three co-authorship walks well inside the 250 ms budget at
20,000 people — introduction paths p95 **31.6 ms** (H1 holds) — and agrees with
Neo4j and a Python breadth-first search on every sampled pair (H2: 0
disagreements). The graph stays in Postgres.

## 4. Interpretation

Plan CN-07 step 5 said to move the co-authorship graph to Neo4j only if a
pre-registered benchmark showed Postgres missing the budget at realistic
size. It does not: the slowest Postgres walk in the pre-registered arm is
31.6 ms at p95, an eighth of the budget, and in the exploratory arm with
prolific co-authors (degree up to 417) it is 60 ms. Neo4j is faster on
introduction paths (5.7 ms vs 31.6 ms at 20,000) and slower on distances in
the hub graph (103 ms vs 60 ms); neither difference matters to a person
waiting on a click. Keeping one store means consent, tiers and deletion never
have to be mirrored into a second database. Recorded as D-007.

## 5. Threats to validity

- **Synthetic graphs.** Lab cliques plus preferential attachment; real
  co-authorship has larger hubs, denser cross-field links, and time. Arm B
  adds hubs, but it is exploratory and not pre-registered.
- **Postgres introduction latency grows with the number of open people.** The
  walk passes every allowed intermediary as an array (5,932 at 20,000) and
  p50 rose from 3.4 ms at 5,000 to 25.9 ms at 20,000. At 100,000 people with
  30% open it could approach the budget; see Q-046.
- **Few reachable targets.** With 30% of people open, only 17–40 of 300
  sampled targets had an allowed path; most introduction queries terminate
  early, which flatters both stores equally.
- **Shared machine** (ISSUES 1) and one run per arm.
- **Neo4j queries were written once**, in the natural Cypher; a tuned plan
  (e.g. separating the predicate from `shortestPath`, as Neo4j's own planner
  notice suggested) could be faster. The comparison is of reasonable, not
  optimal, queries in each store.

## 6. What surprised us

The abandoned first attempt of arm B (ISSUES 3) saw Neo4j distance queries of
about 15 s; the rerun on the same graph finished every query under 154 ms with
no timeouts. The cause was not isolated (a cold page cache after deleting and
reloading 157,000 relationships is the likeliest); it is a reason not to trust
a single slow observation in either direction.
