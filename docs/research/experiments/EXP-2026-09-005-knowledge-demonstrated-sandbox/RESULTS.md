# EXP-2026-09-005 — Results

**System:** `apps/knowledge` (collaboration-network CN-01 and CN-02) at the
commits in `manifest.yaml`. **Data:** a synthetic world — 240 fictional
researchers, 1,099 papers, 48 labs, 48 projects — whose abstracts the local
model wrote. **Judgements:** planted ground truth, not human assessment.
**Runs:** one per arm, plus a six-point threshold sweep and one deterministic
re-link. Every number below is in `data/metrics.json`, written by
`code/analyse.py` from the harness outputs in `data/`.

## 1. Headline

Nothing that must be zero was non-zero in either arm (H1 holds). Asking for
techniques by their standard names (arm B, prompt `w2`) raised the share of
declared methods that meet the person's own papers from **0.658 to 0.775
(+0.117)** and cut method splits from **38 to 16**, so H2 holds; no two planted
methods were ever merged into one concept, at any threshold tried (H3 holds);
the field ruler and Q1 were exact (H4, H5 hold).

## 4. Interpretation

- **The pipeline holds at this scale.** 1,099 papers read with no model
  failure, none thin, every paper with at least one method term naming its real
  method in 99.8% of cases, and a second pass that read nothing and called the
  model zero times. Private words never became shared concepts; conversation
  never appeared; a person's roll-up never cited someone else's paper.
- **The prompt mattered more than the threshold for the first gain.** In `w1`
  the model decorated techniques ("specialized implementation of gradient
  boosting") and listed descriptions as methods; each decoration was its own
  concept. Naming techniques plainly removed 22 of 38 splits.
- **What is left is synonymy, not noise.** Every split remaining at 0.92
  (`tables/splits.md`) is two real names for one technique: "DFT calculations"
  and "density functional theory", "kriging" and "Gaussian process regression",
  "particle filter" and "sequential Monte Carlo". An embedding threshold cannot
  close these safely (Q-043).
- **0.92 is conservative, as intended.** Lowering to 0.85 would have met 0.871
  of declared methods with no measured harm; 0.80 began absorbing distinct
  concepts. The shipped default stays at 0.92 until the concept-merge
  experiment on real data decides (Q-042).

## 5. Threats to validity

- **Circularity.** The model that wrote each abstract also read it, and was
  told to name the method exactly; real abstracts are less cooperative. These
  numbers bound behaviour on clean text; they are not accuracy on the
  literature.
- **Lexical scoring.** Whether a term "names" a planted method is decided by
  fixed fragments (ISSUES.md 5); a correct paraphrase without one would count
  as a miss, and merges among problems and settings are not scored at all —
  which is why the sweep's 0.85 and 0.88 rows say "none *measured*".
- **One world.** One seed, 16 methods, 8 fields. A world with more
  near-miss pairs could find the first wrong merge above 0.80.
- **Arm B changed two things** (the prompt and a generic-term validator) and
  the experiment does not separate them.

## 6. What surprised us

That no planted near miss merged even at 0.80: "Kalman filter" and "particle
filter", "random forest" and "gradient boosting" stayed apart. The first
damage at a low threshold was not between methods at all but between a
person's undeclared method, a project's need, and something nearby — the
places the scorer happened to be looking.
