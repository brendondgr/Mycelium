# EXP-2026-08-004 — Results

> Every number below rests on LongEval-Sci `dctr` qrels: **click-model-derived
> pseudo-relevance, not human assessment**, unioned across each synthetic
> profile's six constituent queries.

## 1. Headline

**Two findings, both negative for the slate, one of them actionable.**

1. **At production settings the slate is completely inert.** With
   `PAPER_MATCH_THRESHOLD = 0.5` and `PAPER_MATCH_MAX_SURFACE = 250`, the slate
   arm and the plain cluster arm produce **identical surfaced sets for 100% of
   profiles**, in all three grouping modes. The 250-item backstop is generous
   relative to a ~575-candidate pool, so every theme's top ten is already inside
   the cut and `top_per_cluster` has nothing to add.
2. **Where the cut does bind, the slate's gain is mostly bought, not earned.**
   At a 30-item cut it raises whole-set theme coverage under `imbalanced` by
   **+0.0667** (95% CI `[+0.0222, +0.1111]`) over the cluster path — but it does
   so by surfacing 33.8 items against 30. Give the cluster path the slate's own
   34 slots and the gain falls to **+0.0333** with a CI spanning zero
   (`[+0.0000, +0.0778]`). Pre-registered falsifier 3 fires.

So **Q-010 is answered "no, not demonstrably."** The slate does not rescue
C-003. The point estimates under `imbalanced` all favour it and the `random`
control is null, so a real effect is not excluded — but it is not resolved at
n = 30, and it is not distinguishable from simply surfacing four more papers.

## 4. Interpretation

### Q-010 — does the slate deliver what global max-pooling did not?

`EXP-2026-08-003` refuted C-003 for the **global** cluster ranking and left this
open, because the lab feed adds a per-theme slate whose own docstring describes
exactly the failure that experiment measured. Three readings:

**The mechanism is real but small.** The slate genuinely changes what is
surfaced — identical-set share is 0–3.3% in `cut30`, so it is not a no-op — and
it rescues a mean of 3.5–3.8 papers per profile that the global cut discarded.
Under `imbalanced` those rescued papers move all three coverage measures in the
right direction at once, including `cluster_gini@1000` **down** 0.0500
(CI `[−0.0851, −0.0188]`, lower is better). That is the exact opposite of what
cluster-scoping did to gini in `EXP-2026-08-003`, where it rose in all nine
cells. So the slate does counteract the concentration the max operator causes.

**But four extra slots do nearly as well.** Under the size-matched condition
every one of those significances evaporates: coverage +0.0333 (spans zero),
`min_cluster_recall@1000` +0.0038 (spans zero), gini −0.0266 (spans zero). A
theme-aware allocation rule that cannot beat "show four more papers" has not
demonstrated that the *theme-awareness* is what helped.

**And the control behaves.** Under `random` grouping the slate's coverage and
gini deltas span zero in both conditions. `min_cluster_recall@1000` is marginally
positive under `random` at unequal size (+0.0051, CI `[+0.0007, +0.0106]`) and
spans zero once size-matched. The one measure where `imbalanced` stands out
cleanly from `random` is gini, and only before size-matching.

### The product finding

`PAPER_MATCH_MAX_SURFACE = 250` makes `top_per_cluster` **dead code for the lab
paper feed** at this pool size. The slate can only act when the surfaced set is
small relative to `clusters × cluster_top_n` (here 3 × 10 = 30); at 250 out of a
~575 pool it never binds. Whatever one concludes about the mechanism, the
deployed configuration is not exercising it.

This is a statement about the *configuration*, not about the code, and it is
scoped to synthetic profiles on LongEval-Sci. It should not be turned into a
settings change without Q-009 evidence on real declared profiles — the pool size
and score distribution on the production corpus differ, and the whole point of
C-008/Q-001 is that production changes are not made on external evidence alone.

### Consequence for C-003

C-003 stays **refuted**. `EXP-2026-08-003` refuted its mechanism for the global
ranking; this experiment shows the lab path's additional mechanism does not
restore it either, at least not measurably. The claim's note is updated to say
both paths were tested rather than one.

## 5. Threats to validity

- **The size-matched control is conservative in one direction.** Giving the
  cluster path 34 slots hands it four *more* of its own top-ranked papers, which
  is the strongest possible use of those slots for relevance and a reasonable one
  for coverage. It is the right comparison for "did the allocation rule earn its
  keep", but a reader who cares only about "is the lab feed better with the slate
  on" should look at the unequal-size rows and treat the extra items as part of
  the treatment.
- **n = 30, and the effects are small.** Two of three coverage measures under
  `imbalanced` had intervals whose lower bound was exactly zero even before
  size-matching. More profiles would resolve this; the construction supports it
  cheaply (`candidate_pool` is 300).
- **`@30` and `@10` are structurally blind here.** The slate's additions sort to
  the bottom by `final_score`, so any top-k at or below the global cut sees a
  delta of exactly 0.0. Those rows are in the table and are all zeros; they are
  not evidence of no effect. See `ISSUES.md` — this was a flaw in the
  pre-registered metric set.
- Everything inherited from `EXP-2026-08-003`: synthetic profiles, union-of-qrels
  (blind to a document relevant only to a *combination* of interests),
  click-derived judgments, one snapshot, `full` corpus scope only.

## 6. What surprised us

**The pre-registered co-primary metric could not observe the mechanism at all.**
`cluster_coverage@30` was chosen before the runs on the reasoning that a slate
allocating 3 × 10 = 30 slots must show up at depth 30. That reasoning was wrong
in a way that is obvious in hindsight: `fuse_clusters_pool` re-sorts the union by
`final_score`, and slate members were by definition *below* the global cut, so
they land at ranks 31+ and no top-30 can ever see them. Every `@10` and `@30`
delta in this experiment is exactly 0.0 for that reason. The flaw was caught from
the surfacing diagnostic before any outcome was known, and is recorded as a
protocol amendment in `ISSUES.md` rather than quietly fixed.

**Production surfaces exactly 250 every time.** Not "about 250" — the backstop
binds on every single profile in every arm, which means the 0.5 cosine threshold
admits more than 250 of a ~575 pool for every synthetic profile tested. If that
holds on the production corpus, the threshold is not doing the work anyone thinks
it is doing either.

## 7. Follow-ups

- **Q-011** — Does the slate's `imbalanced` advantage resolve with more profiles?
  Every point estimate favours it and the control is null, but three of the
  size-matched intervals sit just across zero. This is the cheapest open question
  in the record: raise `n_profiles` and re-run one condition.
- **Q-012** — On the production corpus, does `PAPER_MATCH_THRESHOLD = 0.5` admit
  more than `PAPER_MATCH_MAX_SURFACE` candidates for a typical lab, as it does
  for every synthetic profile here? If so the deployed cut is the backstop rather
  than the threshold, and the slate is inert in production for the same reason.
- Q-009 (real declared clusters) is unchanged and still gates any product action.
