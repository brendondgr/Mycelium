# EXP-2026-08-011 — Results

**Question (`Q-022`):** after two refutations, C-008 and C-014 were the only
positive findings left in the ladder and neither had an interval. Do they survive?

**Answer: yes, both, comfortably.** They are the ladder's surviving positive
results, and they are recall results — not ranking results.

Generated by `code/recall.py`. `longeval-sci-2026/snapshot-1/train`, `dctr` qrels,
100 queries, pooled scope. 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples, fixed seed.

## 1. Reproduction gate

Every arm returned its recorded value to four decimals — `dense_only` 0.6120,
`fusion_dense_pool` 0.5988, `fusion_union_pool` 0.6511,
`fusion_bm25_dense_splade` 0.7141, `bm25_only` 0.6903. The contrasts below are
about the arms they name.

## 2. Both claims survive

**R@100**

| Contrast | claim | mean Δ | 95% CI | excludes 0 | win/loss/tie | sign p |
|---|:--:|---:|---|:--:|---|---:|
| `fusion_union_pool` − `fusion_dense_pool` | **C-008** | +0.0523 | [+0.0235, +0.0858] | **yes** | 28/10/62 | 0.0051 |
| `fusion_bm25_dense_splade` − `fusion_union_pool` | **C-014** | +0.0630 | [+0.0396, +0.0923] | **yes** | 39/3/58 | 5.6e−09 |

Both pass the decision rule as written: interval excludes zero **and** the
per-query record favours the treatment. C-014's record — **39 wins against 3
losses** — is the most consistent effect measured anywhere in this ladder. C-008's
28/10 is weaker but clear.

**And the recall surfaces in the ranking.** A recall gain that never reaches a
reader is worth little, so the same contrasts on nDCG@100:

| Contrast | mean Δ | 95% CI | excludes 0 | win/loss/tie |
|---|---:|---|:--:|---|
| `fusion_union_pool` − `fusion_dense_pool` | +0.0300 | [+0.0155, +0.0455] | **yes** | 59/25/16 |
| `fusion_bm25_dense_splade` − `fusion_union_pool` | +0.0226 | [+0.0082, +0.0374] | **yes** | 62/28/10 |

Both significant, both with strong win records. So these are not bookkeeping gains
in a metric nobody reads — widening the pool genuinely improves the deeper
ranking.

## 3. But neither beats BM25 on *ranking*

The contrasts against `bm25_only`, which is where the ladder's story ends:

| Contrast | metric | mean Δ | 95% CI | excludes 0 |
|---|---|---:|---|:--:|
| `fusion_bm25_dense_splade` − `bm25_only` | R@100 | +0.0238 | [+0.0031, +0.0465] | **yes** |
| `fusion_union_pool` − `bm25_only` | R@100 | **−0.0392** | [−0.0711, −0.0100] | **yes** |
| `fusion_bm25_dense_splade` − `bm25_only` | nDCG@100 | +0.0042 | [−0.0273, +0.0339] | no |
| `fusion_bm25_dense_splade` − `bm25_only` | nDCG@10 | −0.0028 | [−0.0458, +0.0389] | no |
| `fusion_union_pool` − `bm25_only` | nDCG@10 | −0.0156 | [−0.0526, +0.0190] | no |

Three things worth separating.

**Two-channel fusion is significantly *worse* than BM25 on recall** (−0.0392,
interval excludes zero, 14 wins to 32 losses). BM25 alone finds more relevant
documents in the top 100 than BM25 fused with the dense channel does. That is a
sharper statement than anything in `EXP-2026-08-008`, which only ever compared
fusion variants to each other on recall.

**Adding SPLADE reverses that**: the three-channel pool beats BM25 on recall by
+0.0238 with an interval excluding zero. It is the **only configuration measured
in this entire ladder that beats BM25 on any metric with a significant interval.**
Note the sign test is 0.2 (24 wins, 15 losses), so it is a mean-driven effect
rather than a broad one — weaker evidence than C-014's own 39/3.

**Nothing beats BM25 on nDCG@10.** Not the three-channel pool, not the union pool,
not anything tried in `EXP-2026-08-008`/`009`/`010`. Every interval spans zero.

## 4. What the ladder actually established

Reading all four experiments together:

- **Ranking quality: BM25 is the ceiling.** Eleven contrasts across three
  experiments, and not one significantly beats it on nDCG@10.
- **Recall: pool construction matters, and it is the one place effort pays.**
  Production's dense-restricted pool is the worst configuration tested; a union
  pool fixes part of it; adding SPLADE fixes more and overtakes BM25.
- **Every reranking stage tried is neutral or harmful** — ColBERT (refuted),
  cross-encoder (refuted twice, and harmful at n=492), LLM listwise and pointwise
  (no signal, order-dependent, non-discriminating).

The shape of that is coherent rather than accidental: **widening what is retrieved
helps; re-sorting what was retrieved does not.** Every positive result is a recall
result and every refuted claim is a ranking result.

## 5. Threats to validity

1. **One snapshot.** `sci-2026-train` only, 100 queries, click-derived `dctr`
   judgments. The 2025-01 fusion arms need a ~2 h corpus encode and were not run
   here or in `EXP-2026-08-009`. C-008 and C-014 are therefore supported on one
   snapshot with pseudo-relevance judgments — better than no interval, short of
   settled.
2. **Pooled scope.** R@100 is recall *within the 84,489-document subset*. It
   supports a claim about the relative ordering of pool constructions, **not**
   about recall on a production corpus. `Q-001` is untouched.
3. **n = 100.** The recall effects are 3–6× the ±0.04 band that defeated the
   nDCG@10 contrasts, which is why they resolve — but the intervals are still
   wide ([+0.0235, +0.0858] for C-008).
4. **SPLADE's cost is unchanged by this**: 58 minutes of encoding per 84k
   documents and a second index to maintain, for +0.0238 R@100 over BM25 with a
   non-significant sign test.

## 6. What this changes

- **C-008 → `supported`**, now with an interval rather than a point estimate.
- **C-014 → `supported`** (restored from `partial`), with the strongest per-query
  record in the record.
- **A new negative fact worth stating**: two-channel fusion is significantly worse
  than BM25 on recall. The dense channel is not merely failing to help there — it
  is displacing documents BM25 would have found. Recorded as **C-018**.
