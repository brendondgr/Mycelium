## 1. Headline

Nobody was ever findable, or previewed, beyond what their settings and
verified email allow: **0 violations** across 240 scope derivations, 58,794
(seeker, candidate, scope) visibility checks, 28 refused choices, every
expired setting, and 82 previews. The appearance cap held (no one shown to
more than 10 distinct seekers) and cut the concentration of exposure from a
Gini of **0.44 to 0.04** — at the cost of withholding **75%** of would-be
results in this deliberately heavy week.

## 4. Interpretation

The rules that make being findable safe are deterministic, and at this scale
they are exactly right: verified scope cannot be claimed by typing, both
sides must allow a match, choices a person's email cannot support are
refused, a lapsed setting is closed the moment it lapses, and the preview can
only show what a search could match — never conversation, never a project.

The cap does what the design wants — the most sought-after people stop being
flooded — but it is not free. With 2,000 searches over 82 open people in one
week, three results in four were withheld because the person had already been
shown to ten seekers. At realistic demand the share will be far lower; at a
single institution with few open people it could leave seekers with little.
That trade-off belongs to CN-05's load test and to real usage (Q-045).

## 5. Threats to validity

A stand-in search, an invented demand pattern, a reference by the same author,
and a cap that is not transactional (ISSUES.md 1, 2, 4).

## 6. What surprised us

How binding a cap of 10 becomes under Zipf-shaped demand: the uncapped week
put one person in front of 220 of 240 people.
