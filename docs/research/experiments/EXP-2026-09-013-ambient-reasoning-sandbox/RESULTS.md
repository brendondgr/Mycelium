# EXP-2026-09-013 — Results

## 1. Headline

On four weeks of model-written conversation in three fake labs (18 opted-in
people, one who never opted in each, 276 planted statements), Plan 04 kept
every privacy promise (**H1: all eleven harm checks zero**), found 91.7% of
what was planted (H2 holds), and decided the right outcome for 76.1% of
planted topics with **no false suggestion** (H4 holds). **H3 failed as
registered:** the kind of understanding was right for 58.5% of matched
observations (bar 0.80) — the extractor reads a person's own "I'd like to try
X on my work" as a *project* intent and "I've decided against X" as a
*decision* rather than a rejection. Post hoc (runs 4–5, not verdicts): a
sharper prompt plus two deliberation fixes raise kind accuracy to 0.772,
outcome accuracy to 0.894, and keep false suggestions at zero.

## 4. Interpretation

- **The privacy promises held end to end, under the real model.** Nobody who
  had not opted in left a trace; no stored account or explanation quoted a
  message (by the name-aware rule, ISSUES pre-run 4); every body was deleted
  once read; no lab finding rested on a private observation; no explanation
  invented a date. Across five runs, 0 harm-check hits.
- **Reading is good, labelling is the weak part.** The extractor found 91–92%
  of planted statements, from the right speaker, on the right message. What
  it got wrong was *which kind* of thing each was — and the kind decides the
  outcome. Two readings mattered: "decided against X" as a decision, and a
  person's own wish phrased as project talk as a project intent. The first is
  a prompt fix. The second is also a design point: with no project to
  attribute it to, an intention whose subject is the speaker is theirs.
- **The decision rules are conservative in the right direction.** No run
  suggested a one-off, a colleague's idea, a rejected or a declared topic
  once the fixes were in; the misses are topics that *should* have been
  suggested and were not (capabilities read as project work: 8 of 18 in run
  5). For a feature whose failure mode is asking people about things they do
  not care about, missing is the cheaper error.
- **Fixed, with tests:** the kind prompt (`a4`), unattributed self intents as
  interests, and a retired row never counting as live (run 4's regression).

## 5. Threats to validity

- **The conversations are model-written.** The generator was told to say each
  plant in the speaker's own words; it often phrased a personal wish as
  project talk ("for my next project"), which is realistic but is also the
  generator's habit, not a sample of real labs. Real people will phrase things
  differently; the pilot (Plan 10, measure 1) is the real test.
- **The kind measure is strict.** A plant "says they would like to try X on
  their work" counts as right only when read as an interest; reading it as a
  project intent is scored wrong though it is a defensible reading.
- **Truth depends on the generator.** 12 of 288 plants were left out by the
  generator and dropped from the truth; 129 of 1,270 messages were under the
  24-character floor and never recorded (none of them a plant).
- **One seed, one world, one night of deciding.** Deliberation ran once over
  the whole four weeks; nightly reruns (reinforcement, declines resting) are
  covered by unit tests, not here.

## 6. What surprised us

- The harness, not the pipeline, lost the text in run 2: Django's local-memory
  cache culls past 300 entries. Recorded in ISSUES.
- 157 of 365 observations were project developments, many from filler
  logistics — which is why an unresolved project topic is only asked about
  when it passes the full consistency test (a smoke-run change).
