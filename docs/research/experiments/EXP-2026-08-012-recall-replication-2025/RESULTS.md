# EXP-2026-08-012 — Results

**Question (`Q-024`):** do C-008, C-014 and C-018 replicate on `sci-2025-01` —
492 queries, released human assessments — or were they artefacts of one snapshot
with click-derived judgments?

**Answer: all three replicate, and one adjacent claim does not.** The
pool-construction ordering is now the most robust result in the programme. The
statement that the three-channel pool *beats BM25* is withdrawn.

Generated by `code/replicate.py`; see [`tables/replication.md`](tables/replication.md).

## 1. All three pre-registered claims replicate

**R@100**

| Claim | Contrast | 2026 (100q, `dctr`) | 2025-01 (492q, `test`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C-008** | union − dense pool | +0.0523 [+0.0235, +0.0858] · 28W/10L | **+0.2459** [+0.2196, +0.2717] · **302W/21L**, p=6e−65 |
| **C-014** | +SPLADE − union | +0.0630 [+0.0396, +0.0923] · 39W/3L | **+0.0330** [+0.0231, +0.0433] · 106W/26L, p=1e−12 |
| **C-018** | union − BM25 | −0.0392 [−0.0711, −0.0100] · 14W/32L | **−0.0219** [−0.0338, −0.0104] · 48W/94L, p=1e−4 |

Same sign, intervals excluding zero, per-query records on the same side — the
decision rule in full, on both snapshots and under both judgment regimes. That is
a materially stronger footing than agreement within one.

**The pre-registered secondary prediction holds.** C-008's effect is ~5× larger
here (+0.2459 against +0.0523), and the stated reason was recorded in advance:
this claim is about what the dense-restricted pool costs *when the dense channel
is weak*, and it is far weaker on this snapshot (`dense_only` R@100 0.3348 against
0.6120 on 2026). 302 wins to 21 losses is the most lopsided result anywhere in
this work.

**And on this snapshot the gains reach the visible ranking.** On 2026 both claims
were recall-only — their nDCG@10 contrasts spanned zero. Here C-008 moves nDCG@10
by +0.0369 and C-014 by +0.0147, both with intervals excluding zero, and nDCG@100
by +0.0990 and +0.0186. So the pool fixes are not merely repositioning documents
inside a metric nobody reads.

**C-018 was exploratory and is now pre-registered and replicated.** Two-channel
fusion really does retrieve *less* than BM25 alone, and here the damage also
reaches the ranking (−0.0498 nDCG@100, −0.0666 nDCG@10, both significant).

## 2. What does **not** replicate — and it is a claim I made

`EXP-2026-08-011` reported that the three-channel pool was *"the only
configuration in the entire ladder that beats `bm25_only` on any metric with an
interval excluding zero"* (+0.0238 R@100 `[+0.0031, +0.0465]`).

**It does not hold here.**

| Contrast | 2026 | 2025-01 | replicates |
|---|---|---|:--:|
| `fusion_bm25_dense_splade` − `bm25_only`, R@100 | +0.0238 [+0.0031, +0.0465] | **+0.0111 [−0.0020, +0.0244]** | **NO** |

The interval now spans zero and the sign test was never significant on either
snapshot (p = 0.2 and p = 0.13). The 2026 result sat barely outside zero on its
lower bound; this is what a marginal interval looks like when it is retested.

Worse for that reading: on the ranking metrics the three-channel pool is
**significantly worse** than BM25 here — −0.0312 nDCG@100 and −0.0519 nDCG@10,
where on 2026 both spanned zero. In absolute terms, BM25 scores 0.2486 nDCG@10 on
this snapshot and the best fusion arm 0.1967.

**So the claim is withdrawn**: no configuration measured in this programme
reliably beats BM25 on anything.

## 3. The two statements this leaves, and they are different

The distinction matters and is easy to blur:

1. **Within the fusion architecture, pool construction is robustly worth fixing.**
   dense-pool → union → union+SPLADE improves recall on both snapshots, under both
   judgment regimes, with large per-query margins. This is settled as far as this
   collection can settle anything.
2. **The fusion architecture as a whole does not beat BM25**, and on the
   human-judged snapshot it is clearly behind on ranking. C-010 stands refuted and
   is now refuted more firmly than before.

Statement 1 is what to act on if you are running fusion — which Mycelium is,
because the dense channel is what makes profile-level matching possible at all and
BM25 cannot be run against a profile embedding. Statement 2 is what stops anyone
reading statement 1 as "our stack is winning".

## 5. Threats to validity

1. **Still the wrong task.** LongEval is `query → documents`; Mycelium is
   `declared-interest profile → feed`. Two snapshots of the wrong task remain the
   wrong task. **`Q-001` is the gate on any production change and this experiment
   does not move it.**
2. **Snapshot and judgment provenance vary together** (ISSUES.md §2). Agreement
   across both is *stronger* evidence than agreement within one; a disagreement —
   as with the withdrawn claim in §2 — is ambiguous between the two causes.
3. **Pooled scope** on both snapshots. R@100 is recall within each pooled subset.
4. **The magnitude is snapshot-dependent even where the sign is not**: C-008 is
   +0.0523 on one snapshot and +0.2459 on the other. The *direction* replicates;
   the effect size is a property of how weak the dense channel is on that corpus,
   which is exactly what the claim says and exactly why it cannot be quoted as a
   single number.
