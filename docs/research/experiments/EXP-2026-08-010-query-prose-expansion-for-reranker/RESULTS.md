# EXP-2026-08-010 — Results

**Question:** `EXP-2026-08-009` found the cross-encoder's damage concentrated on
short queries and absent at 5+ words. Does giving the *reranker* an LLM-written
prose form of the query — while BM25 still retrieves with the original short
string — recover the loss, and does recovery scale with prose length?

**Answer: no, and emphatically the opposite.** Every expansion length is worse
than the raw query, monotonically, and the pre-registered hypothesis is falsified.
Query length was a **correlate, not a cause**.

Tables generated by `code/analyse.py` and `code/language_check.py`. Snapshot
`longeval-sci/2025-01`, released `test` qrels, 492 queries, pooled scope.

## 1. More prose is monotonically worse

| Arm | nDCG@10 | Δ vs BM25 | 95% CI | excludes 0 | win/loss/tie | sign p |
|---|---:|---:|---|:--:|---|---:|
| `bm25_only` (baseline) | 0.2486 | — | — | — | — | — |
| xenc, raw query | 0.2047 | −0.0439 | [−0.0597, −0.0283] | yes | 139/218/135 | 3.4e−05 |
| xenc, ~8w prose | 0.1696 | −0.0790 | [−0.0980, −0.0606] | yes | 118/251/123 | 3.7e−12 |
| xenc, ~16w prose | 0.1611 | −0.0875 | [−0.1067, −0.0683] | yes | 110/263/119 | 1.4e−15 |
| xenc, ~20w prose | 0.1564 | −0.0921 | [−0.1113, −0.0728] | yes | 112/256/124 | 4.3e−14 |
| xenc, ~40w prose | 0.1547 | −0.0939 | [−0.1133, −0.0742] | yes | 110/256/126 | 1.6e−14 |

Adding prose **doubles** the damage: −0.0439 with the raw query becomes −0.0939 at
40 words. The trend is monotone across all four lengths, every interval excludes
zero, and every sign test is overwhelming. The falsifier written in `PROTOCOL.md`
— "the ~40-word arm remains significantly below `bm25_only`, or the trend is flat
or non-monotonic" — fires on both counts.

**The correctness check passes:** `R@100` is 0.5972 in every arm, identical to
`bm25_only`. Reranking a fixed top-50 cannot change what was retrieved, and it
didn't — so the intervention really was confined to the reranker's input.

## 2. It also broke the one bucket that was working

Δ vs BM25 split by the length of the **original** query:

| Arm | 1 word (n=231) | 2 words (n=181) | 3–4 words (n=48) | 5+ words (n=32) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| xenc, raw query | −0.0422 | −0.0401 | −0.0969 | **+0.0016** |
| xenc, ~8w prose | −0.0597 | −0.0944 | −0.1437 | −0.0344 |
| xenc, ~16w prose | −0.0689 | −0.1047 | −0.1329 | −0.0566 |
| xenc, ~20w prose | −0.0698 | −0.1065 | −0.1708 | −0.0539 |
| xenc, ~40w prose | −0.0717 | −0.1062 | −0.1800 | −0.0552 |

This is the sharpest disproof in the table. If length were the mechanism,
expansion should have lifted the short buckets toward the long one. Instead it
**pushed the 5+ word bucket down from +0.0016 to −0.0552** — it damaged the only
queries that were previously unharmed, the ones that already had the natural-prose
length the intervention was trying to manufacture.

Harm grows in every bucket, and grows *fastest* at 2 words (−0.0401 → −0.1062) and
3–4 words (−0.0969 → −0.1800). Those are the buckets whose original queries are
short but unambiguous — precisely where a rare discriminative term does the most
work and where diluting it into general prose costs the most.

## 3. Nor is it a language problem

The corpus is multilingual (`Burkulması Engellenmiş Çelik Çaprazların Uç
Stabilitelerinin Arttırılması` is a real title in the frozen subset) while the
reranker is English-only, which is an obvious second candidate. It is also wrong.
Δ split by the share of each query's candidate set that is not predominantly
Latin-script:

| non-Latin share of the top-50 | queries | mean Δ nDCG@10 | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0% non-Latin | 277 | **−0.0493** | [−0.0714, −0.0279] |
| 1–10% | 139 | −0.0389 | [−0.0702, −0.0085] |
| 10–25% | 27 | −0.0363 | [−0.0769, +0.0012] |
| >25% | 43 | **−0.0361** | [−0.0833, +0.0112] |

Harm is **largest where every document is Latin-script** and smallest where
non-Latin documents are most common — the exact opposite of the prediction.
Script is only a proxy for language, but the prediction was directional and the
direction is inverted, which no amount of proxy noise explains away.

## 4. What is left

Two mechanisms proposed, two refuted. The honest conclusion is the plain one:
**this cross-encoder is simply a worse ranker than BM25 on this collection**, and
the short-query pattern in `EXP-2026-08-009` was a correlate of something not yet
identified rather than the cause.

What that leaves standing as the most likely remaining explanation, untested here:
domain mismatch. `ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` is trained on web search over web text;
this is scientific titles and abstracts. That is the same explanation that already
covered SPLADE's failure in `EXP-2026-08-008` — a second MS MARCO-trained model
failing on the same collection, in the same direction, is a pattern rather than a
coincidence. It is exactly what `Q-021` proposes to test with a stronger and
differently-trained reranker.

## 5. A finding worth keeping regardless

**Query expansion for a reranker is actively harmful here, and the harm scales
with how much you expand.** That is useful independently of the cross-encoder
question, because query expansion is a standard recommendation — the source
document that started this work recommends the doc2query direction and treats
HyDE-style query expansion as an open question. On this collection, in the
reranker position, the expansion direction is not merely neutral: it costs more
than the reranker itself did.

Note carefully what is *not* tested: this expands the **query** for the
**reranker**. It says nothing about document-side expansion (doc2query) at ingest,
which is a different intervention with different failure modes.

## 6. Threats to validity

1. **Length and register are confounded.** An expansion is not the query plus
   words; it is a different register ("This search targets…"). No length-matched
   uninformative control was run, so "prose of this length hurts" is measured and
   "more words hurt" is not (ISSUES.md §4).
2. **One expansion prompt, one LLM** (`skynet`, upstream not pinned), **one
   reranker, one snapshot.**
3. **Ambiguity was recorded in advance as a likely failure**, and the 1-word
   bucket does degrade — but so does every other bucket, so ambiguity cannot be
   the whole story.
4. **Pooled scope, re-ranking evaluation**, released `test` qrels.
5. **Wrong task.** LongEval is `query → documents`; Mycelium is
   `declared-interest profile → feed`. `Q-001` untouched.

## 7. What this changes

- **C-017 — refuted** (new). Prose expansion of the query does not recover the
  cross-encoder's short-query loss; it deepens it, monotonically in length.
- **C-013 stays refuted**, and the diagnosis behind it is now firmer: the failure
  is not about query length and not about document language.
- **The practical recommendation is unchanged and reinforced:** do not put this
  cross-encoder in the pipeline, and do not reach for query expansion to rescue it.
- **`Q-021` is now the load-bearing open question.** Two MS MARCO-trained models
  have now failed on this collection in the same direction. Either that family
  does not transfer to scientific abstracts, or both failures are about model
  capacity — and only a stronger, differently-trained reranker distinguishes them.
