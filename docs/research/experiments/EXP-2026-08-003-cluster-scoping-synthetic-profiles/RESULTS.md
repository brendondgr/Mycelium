# EXP-2026-08-003 — Results

> Every number below rests on LongEval-Sci `dctr` qrels: **click-model-derived
> pseudo-relevance, not human assessment**, unioned across each synthetic
> profile's six constituent queries.

## 1. Headline

**C-003 is refuted.** In the one condition it claims to fix — `imbalanced`
grouping, where one theme's vocabulary sits far closer to the corpus than the
others' — cluster-scoped aggregation *lowers* the worst-served theme's recall by
**−0.0032 `min_cluster_recall@10`** (95% CI `[−0.0066, −0.0007]`, paired
bootstrap over 30 profiles), and lowers `cluster_coverage@10` by 0.044 at every
β. Under `coherent` grouping the effect is a statistically indistinguishable
+0.0010, and `cluster_gini@10` — the evenness measure — rises (worsens) under
clustering in all nine cells.

The hypothesis did not survive in any part: not the direction, not the
`imbalanced` prediction, and not the prediction that the gap widens with β (the
primary delta is invariant in β, for the reason in §6).

## 4. Interpretation

### C-003 → refuted

> *"For a lab or broad-interest user, ranking within research clusters and
> max-pooling across them prevents one vocabulary-dominant theme from
> monopolizing the feed, relative to global ranking."*

Three independent readings of the data contradict it:

1. **Direction, in the target condition.** Under `imbalanced` grouping — the
   vocabulary-dominance condition the claim names — clustering makes the
   worst-served theme *worse*, and the interval excludes zero at every β.
   `cluster_coverage@10` also falls, by 0.044 at every β.
2. **Magnitude, in the assumed condition.** Under `coherent` grouping the effect
   is +0.0010 with an interval whose lower bound is exactly zero. Pre-registered
   falsifier 1 fires.
3. **Evenness.** `cluster_gini@10` — the metric that directly measures whether
   one theme takes a disproportionate share — is **higher** (worse) under
   clustering in all nine cells. Max-pooling concentrates the feed rather than
   spreading it.

Reading 3 explains readings 1 and 2. Max-over-clusters is a *winner-takes-most*
operator: a candidate's score is its best cluster's score, so the theme whose
candidates score highest anywhere dominates the global ordering. Flat combination
at least averages a candidate's evidence across every theme it touches. The
mechanism C-003 assumed — that scoping the breadth bonus inside a cluster
protects the weak theme — is real, but it is dominated by the max across
clusters, which does the opposite.

### The negative control fired, and what it means

Pre-registered falsifier 2: *"the harness is wrong, not the finding, if clustered
beats flat under `random` grouping by a comparable margin."*

On the primary metric at *k* = 10, `random` behaves as a null: both deltas span
zero. But at *k* = 100, and on `R@100`, `random` shows the **largest** clustered
gain of any mode (`min_cluster_recall@100` +0.0176, CI `[+0.0011, +0.0372]`;
`R@100` +0.0421, CI `[+0.0262, +0.0593]`) while `coherent` and `imbalanced` span
zero or gain less. The falsifier's condition is met on that family, so it was
investigated before anything here was interpreted.

**The cause is understood and is not a harness defect.** Max-over-clusters
approximates *"score by the single best-matching interest"*, and it approximates
it more completely the more diverse each cluster is. Random clusters span
unrelated interests, so the max across three random clusters is close to the max
across all six interests. Coherent clusters contain near-duplicate interests, so
each cluster's internal combination behaves like one interest and the max across
them is a coarser approximation. The `random` arm is therefore not measuring
noise — it is measuring *maximum-granularity max-pooling*, which is the
better-known operator (TPMS, Charlin & Zemel 2013). The control did its job
exactly as intended: it separates the gain attributable to the **max operator**
from any gain attributable to **cluster semantics**, and all of it turns out to
belong to the operator.

The consequence for this experiment's conclusion is narrow and one-directional.
It permits the negative finding — cluster semantics contribute nothing that
plain max-pooling does not — and it forbids reading the `random` gains as support
for anything. No positive claim is made from them here; the residual question is
logged as Q-007.

### C-002 — no evidence either way

The β axis was included partly to bear on C-002 (the breadth bonus). It does not.
Raising β from 0 to 0.3 changes both arms' absolute scores (flat `nDCG@10` falls
0.1684 → 0.1442 under `coherent`), but the *paired* primary delta is invariant in
β for the mechanical reason in §6. C-002 remains `unsupported`; this experiment
adds nothing to it, and the manifest does not claim it.

## 5. Threats to validity

- **The profiles are synthetic.** Six LongEval keyword queries grouped by
  embedding similarity are not a researcher's declared interests. This tests the
  aggregation math under known, controlled conditions. It does **not** test
  whether real users have cluster-shaped interests, and a refutation here is a
  refutation of the mechanism, not proof that the feature harms real users.
- **`min_cluster_recall@10` is thinly populated.** It is nonzero for only 6–10 of
  30 profiles, and the arms differ on just 1 (`coherent`) or 4 (`imbalanced`) of
  them — see §6. The `imbalanced` interval excludes zero, but it rests on four
  profiles all moving the same way. The `@100` variant is far better populated
  and shows the same absence of a `coherent`/`imbalanced` effect, which is why
  both cut-offs are reported.
- **Union-of-qrels is an approximation.** A document relevant to the
  *combination* of two interests but to neither alone is invisible to it, and
  that is exactly the kind of document a multi-interest profile should reward.
- **Judgments are click-derived**, not human assessment.
- **One collection, one snapshot.** `longeval-sci-2026/snapshot-1/train`, corpus
  scope `full`. Nothing here is compared across corpus scopes.
- **Three hand-rolled metrics.** Comparable within this experiment; not
  comparable to any published number. See `ISSUES.md`.

## 6. What surprised us

**The primary delta is exactly invariant in β**, to six decimal places, within
each grouping mode. That looked like an aliasing bug and was checked directly
against the per-profile records before anything was written.

It is real, and the reason is a genuine limitation of the metric rather than of
the code. `min_cluster_recall@10` is a *minimum over three clusters* of a recall
computed at depth 10, and `cluster_coverage@10` is ≈ 0.5–0.65 — about half the
clusters get nothing in the top 10 at all, so the minimum is zero for most
profiles. The mean delta is therefore carried by a handful of profiles: exactly
one (`coherent-023`) under `coherent`, and four (`imbalanced-011/012/013/020`)
under `imbalanced`. Those same profiles move the same way at every β, so the
mean delta repeats. A metric whose aggregate is decided by four of thirty
profiles is a weak instrument, and that is worth knowing before it is used again.

**Max-pooling improves recall regardless of what the clusters mean.** `R@100`
rises significantly under clustering in seven of nine cells, most under `random`.
The aggregation operator has a real and consistent effect on retrieval; it simply
has nothing to do with the research themes the product asks users to declare.
That is the opposite of the shape C-003 predicted, and it is the most interesting
thing in this experiment.

**The construction was not degenerate.** Falsifier 4 did not fire: flat and
clustered produce identical top-10 lists for 10–30% of profiles under
`coherent`/`random`, and 50–60% under `imbalanced` — well under the 90% threshold.
The abstention-floor histogram in `ISSUES.md` shows 5–24% of surfaced candidates
clearing two or more interests/clusters, so cross-cluster evidence exists and
aggregation had something to work with.

## 7. Follow-ups

Copied into `../../OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

- **Q-007** — If the gain belongs to the max operator rather than to cluster
  semantics, where on the granularity dial (1 cluster = flat mean … *N* clusters
  = TPMS) does retrieval peak, and does the optimum rise with how multi-strand
  the profile is? This is the one question the `random` control makes *more*
  interesting rather than less, and it is the reframing under which a stage-2
  reviewer-expertise experiment would still be worth running.
- **Q-008** — Is `min_cluster_recall@k` the right primary metric, given that its
  aggregate here is decided by four of thirty profiles? A depth-normalised or
  rank-weighted per-cluster measure might have usable power at *k* = 10.
- **Q-009** — Does the refutation survive on profiles whose interests are
  declared rather than synthesised? The mechanism is refuted under controlled
  conditions; the product decision (whether to keep `fuse_clusters_pool` on the
  lab feed) needs evidence on real lab profiles, which this experiment cannot
  supply.

### Consequence for the planned stage 2

The design plan gated a reviewer-expertise experiment
(`cluster-granularity-reviewer-expertise`, ~3 weeks, external data acquisition)
on this result: *"If not, C-003 is refuted — record it and reconsider whether
stage 2 is worth three weeks."* C-003 is refuted, so stage 2 **is not started as
specified**. If it is revived, it should be scoped to Q-007 — the granularity
dial — rather than to C-003, because C-003's specific mechanism is now measured
and does not hold.
