# EXP-2026-08-013 — Results

**Question (`Q-001`):** does the union pool's advantage survive on the
**production** corpus, where documents are longer and queries are declared
interests rather than keywords?

**Answer, in three parts, and they point in different directions:**

1. **The mechanism is live.** On the real store, against the real declared
   interests, the dense-restricted pool leaves a median **40%** of the
   corpus-wide BM25 top-100 unreachable — and the deficit grows with corpus
   size, exactly as the arithmetic predicts. On the deployed opportunity feeds
   it is worst (median reachability 0.44).
2. **But it cannot reach the reader as the code stands.** Across **126
   (regime, target) pairs and all four union arms, the number of union-only
   documents that survive into the surfaced feed is 0** — every one of them. The
   feed's surfacing gate compares `best_cosine` against the match threshold, and
   `best_cosine` is populated only by the dense channel, so a candidate the
   lexical channel found is judged at 0.0 and dropped. **The C-008 fix is a
   two-part change, and shipping only the pool half is provably a no-op.**
3. **Half of `Q-001`'s premise is false.** Declared interests are *not* a
   different query shape from LongEval keywords — median 3 words, **95% at four
   words or fewer**. And production documents are not categorically longer:
   papers run 239 tokens against LongEval's 161–185, while grants (116) and
   conferences (86) are markedly *shorter*.

Generated by [`code/run.py`](code/run.py), [`code/analyse.py`](code/analyse.py)
and [`code/premise.py`](code/premise.py); tables in
[`tables/analysis.md`](tables/analysis.md) and

## 0. Gate

`embeddings.embed_text` over a stored item's own text reproduced that item's
stored `ContentItem.embedding` to **cosine 1.0000000000**. The query side and
the corpus side are the same model, so every dense pool below is the pool
production would have built. Arm A1 calls `_build_pool` directly rather than
reimplementing it.

## 1. Is there anything for a wider pool to reach?

`bm25_reachability@100` — of the corpus-wide BM25 top-100 for one declared
interest, the fraction already inside the dense pool the deployed builder would
have used. `1.0` means the fix is inert.

| Group | n | median | mean | 95% CI | pre-registered verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|:--:|
| all regimes, all targets | 795 | 0.595 | 0.575 | [0.563, 0.587] | between |
| **deployed regimes (R1+R3)** | 757 | **0.610** | 0.583 | [0.571, 0.595] | **between** |
| deployed regimes, real targets only | 697 | 0.630 | 0.603 | [0.592, 0.615] | between |
| R1 papers, day-scoped (deployed) | 646 | 0.640 | 0.600 | [0.587, 0.613] | between |
| **R3 opportunities (deployed)** | 111 | **0.440** | 0.482 | [0.455, 0.511] | **live** |
| R2 papers, corpus-wide (not deployed) | 38 | 0.490 | 0.427 | [0.389, 0.461] | live |

**Read the verdict column as written, not as it would be convenient.** The
pre-registered rule declares *inert* at ≥ 0.90 and *live* at ≤ 0.50, with a
deliberate gap between them. The deployed **paper** feed lands in that gap at
0.61 and the rule says so: the mechanism exists there, but not at the strength
that was pre-declared as decisive. The deployed **opportunity** feeds do clear
the bar at 0.44, and they are where the dense-restricted pool costs most.

Nowhere is it close to inert. The most favourable regime measured anywhere — a
303-document gather day, the smallest batch in the store — is 0.81.

**It is a function of corpus size, and that was predicted before the run.** A
fixed 100-per-interest dense pull covers a small batch and cannot cover a
corpus, so reachability falls as scope grows: 0.81 at 303 documents, 0.64
around 3,000, 0.49 over the whole 35,205-paper store. See
[`fig-reachability-vs-corpus`](figures/fig-reachability-vs-corpus.svg). This is
why the answer differs between Mycelium's two feeds rather than being one
number: the paper feed is scoped to a single gather day and the opportunity
feeds are not.

The opportunity corpora sit **below** the paper trend at matched size — grants
0.43 at 2,171 documents where papers score 0.66–0.69 at 2,000–3,000. Their
documents are also the shortest in the store (86–116 tokens), which is the
obvious candidate explanation and is not tested here.

The pre-registered sensitivity split moves nothing: real targets 0.630 against
0.610 for all six.

## 2. What actually reaches the reader — the finding that decides the engineering

Entrants are documents the union arm surfaces that the deployed arm never did,
summed over all 126 (regime, target) pairs.

| Arm | entrants, deployed gate | **of which union-only** | entrants, gate completed | of which union-only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A2p union (shippable) | 419 | **0** | 7,866 | 7,440 |
| A2 union (harness arm) | 22 | **0** | 3,912 | 3,885 |
| A4 union via FTS (OR) | 407 | **0** | 6,100 | 5,686 |
| A3 union via FTS (`websearch`) | 23 | **0** | 358 | 328 |

**Zero, in every arm, in every pair.** `fuse_interests_pool` surfaces a
candidate only if `best_cosine[id] >= match_threshold`, and `_build_pool`
populates `best_cosine` solely from the dense channel's hits. A document the
lexical channel contributed has no entry, defaults to `0.0`, and cannot clear a
threshold of 0.5 (papers) or 0.4 (opportunities). `fuse_clusters_pool` applies
the identical gate, so this is not a property of the flat path.

This was measured rather than argued: the column is a count taken from the runs,
not a deduction from reading the code.

Complete the gate — compute the cosine for every pool member, which any real fix
must do — and **7,440 union-only documents surface** across the 126 pairs, about
59 per pair against a median surfaced list of 150. So the pool half of the fix
is not merely necessary-but-insufficient; on its own it is inert, and paired
with the gate fix it is a large change to the feed.

Note the 419 entrants A2p does produce under the deployed gate. They are **all**
documents already in the dense pool, re-ranked into the surfaced set by the
wider pool's effect on the fusion scores. Widening the pool changes the ranking
of what was already reachable even today; it just cannot add anything.

## 3. `Q-018`, answered in passing

`Q-018` asked how many candidates `fuse_interest` emits per target on the
production corpus, and whether the 0.15 interest floor is the binding cut.

| Quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| dense pool per target | median 170 (100–403) |
| emitted per interest after the 0.15 floor | median 113 (p10 61, p90 128) |
| share of the target's pool surviving one interest's floor | **61%** |
| surfaced per target after the cosine threshold | median 150 (max 382) |
| pairs hitting the 250 `PAPER_MATCH_MAX_SURFACE` backstop | 3/126 — **1/102** in the deployed paper feed |
| pool growth, union over dense | 1.54× |
| share of the union pool the dense channel never saw | 35% |

**The floor is not the binding cut in production, and that differs from
LongEval.** `EXP-2026-08-008` measured 59–97 candidates emitted from a
200-document pool (≈37% survival); here 61% survives. The cut that binds is the
cosine threshold, which takes a median 170-candidate pool down to 150 surfaced —
and the 250 backstop almost never fires. So on this store
`PAPER_MATCH_THRESHOLD` is the real constraint on the feed, not
`PAPER_MATCH_MAX_SURFACE` and not the interest floor.

That also **answers `Q-012` in the opposite direction to its guess.** `Q-012`
asked whether the threshold admits more candidates than the 250 backstop allows,
which would make the backstop the deployed cut and `top_per_cluster` dead code
for the lab paper feed. In the deployed paper regime it does **not**: the
backstop binds in **1 of 102** (regime, target) pairs, with a median of 148
candidates surfaced. The threshold is the cut.

## 4. The FTS channel Mycelium already runs cannot be reused as-is

A3 tested the corpus-wide lexical channel `apps/content/search.py` runs for
`/api/search/` — Postgres FTS over `ContentItem.search_vector` — because it is
the implementation a production change would reach for first (the index exists;
`rank_bm25` over 35,205 documents in-process does not scale).

**728 of 798 interest queries (91%) return nothing at all.** `websearch` ANDs
unquoted terms, and `search_interests` appends the profile's shared keyword
suffix to every area, so a query like *"entity resolution"* plus nine keywords
demands that all eleven terms appear in one document. That is correct for a
query somebody typed and wrong for a declared interest.

The same index under OR semantics is fine: 4 of 798 empty, median reachability
0.540 — close to BM25Okapi's 0.595 and behind it by roughly one interest in
twenty. So the index is usable and the **query construction** is not. This is a
statement about reusing that channel for the feed; `/api/search/` itself is fed
user-typed queries and is not implicated.

## 5. The premise check — and one half of `Q-001` does not survive it

| Corpus | median tokens | p10 | p90 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| production papers | 239 | 21 | 361 |
| production fellowships | 187 | 120 | 304 |
| production grants | 116 | 89 | 138 |
| production conferences | 86 | 59 | 115 |
| LongEval `sci-2026-train` | 161 | 57 | 331 |
| LongEval `sci-2025-01` | 185 | 15 | 374 |

| Query set | n | median words | 1–2 words | 3–4 | 5+ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **production declared interests** | 38 | **3** | **45%** | **50%** | **5%** |
| LongEval `sci-2026-train` | 100 | 3 | 32% | 33% | 35% |
| LongEval `sci-2025-01` | 492 | 2 | 84% | 10% | 6% |

`Q-001` was posed as a question about a corpus "where documents are longer and
queries are declared interests rather than keywords". **Neither clause holds as
a difference in kind.** Production papers are ~1.4× LongEval's median length —
the same order, not another regime — and two of the three opportunity corpora
are *shorter* than LongEval. Declared interests sit between the two LongEval
query sets in length and inside both of their ranges.

That cuts two ways and both are worth stating:

- **It strengthens the transfer of the LongEval results**, including C-008
  itself. The task shape objection that has qualified every number in this
  record is smaller than it was assumed to be *on these two axes*. It is not
  gone: `profile → feed` fuses several interests into one ranked list and
  LongEval never does, and there are still no relevance labels here.
- **It hardens C-013's refutation for production.** The cross-encoder was
  measured significantly harmful at 1–4 word queries and roughly neutral at 5+
  (`EXP-2026-08-009`, `EXP-2026-08-010`). **95% of real declared interests are
  four words or fewer.** Mycelium's queries live almost entirely inside the harm
  band, so "do not add a cross-encoder" now rests on production-shaped evidence
  and not only on external-collection evidence.

## 6. What this does and does not settle for `Q-001`

**Settled.** The mechanism half. There is a large, systematic set of documents
the deployed pool cannot reach on real declared interests, it is not an artefact
of LongEval, and its size is a predictable function of corpus scope. The
inert branch of the pre-registered rule did **not** fire.

**Not settled, and not moved at all.** Whether reaching them helps anybody.
There are no relevance judgments for this corpus — W5 remains not started, and
the store's behavioural signal is 9 saves, 0 collection items, 0 ignores, 2
relevance events, none of which can carry a recall measurement. The 4,588
`RelevanceScore` rows are the deployed LLM's own opinion of its own feed and were
deliberately not used.

So: `Q-001` is **half answered**. The half that could be answered without labels
is answered; the half that decides whether to ship is exactly the half W5 gates.

**And a production change was not authorised by this.** What the experiment does
authorise is a sharper statement of what the change *is*: two edits, not one, and
the pool edit alone is measurably worthless.

## 7. Threats to validity

1. **n = 6 targets, 38 interests, one development store.** Declared in advance
   and unfixable here: the store has six users, some of them fixtures. The
   real-target split moves nothing, but 4 targets is not a defence.
2. **No relevance labels** — §6. Everything above is about what is *reachable*
   and *surfaceable*, never about what is *good*.
3. **The store is a month stale** (freshest gather day 2026-07-20) and its gather
   batches, 303–4,475 papers, set the R1 corpus sizes. A deployment ingesting
   more per day would sit further left on the reachability curve — i.e. worse.
4. **The 17 R1 regimes are not independent.** They share the same 38 interests
   and overlapping corpora characteristics, so the day-to-day spread understates
   the true uncertainty. They are shown as a curve against corpus size for that
   reason and never pooled into one interval.
5. **A2's lexical channel is truncated at `pool_per_interest` and A1's is not**
   (ISSUES §2) — inherited deliberately from the harness arm so the contrast is
   the same contrast C-008 was measured with. A2p is the untruncated variant and
   is the one quoted for the surfacing result.
6. **Reachability is a level, not a contrast.** `paired_bootstrap` is used on it
   as a one-sample resample; there is no baseline arm to pair against, because
   pairing requires a relevance measure.

## 8. What this changes

- **`Q-001` → half answered.** The mechanism half is settled on production; the
  value half is unchanged and still gated on W5. It is no longer the *only*
  thing between the programme and a production decision — W5 is.
- **`Q-018` → answered.** The 0.15 interest floor is **not** the binding cut in
  production (61% survives, against ≈37% on LongEval). `PAPER_MATCH_THRESHOLD`
  is.
- **`Q-012` → answered, against its own guess.** The 250 `MAX_SURFACE` backstop
  fires in 3 of 126 pairs; the match threshold is the deployed cut.
- **New claim C-019** — widening the retrieval pool cannot change the surfaced
  feed while `best_cosine` is populated only by the dense channel. Supported,
  0/126 across four arms.
- **New claim C-020** — on the production corpus and real declared interests the
  dense-restricted pool leaves a substantial share of the lexical top-100
  unreachable, growing with corpus scope. Supported for the mechanism; silent on
  value.
- **C-013's refutation is hardened for production**: 95% of real declared
  interests fall in the query-length band where the cross-encoder was measured
  harmful.
- **A note added to C-008**: the production corpus is no longer untested for the
  mechanism, and the "documents are longer / queries are different" premise that
  qualified it is measured and largely does not hold.
