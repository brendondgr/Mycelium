# EXP-2026-11-001 — Results

## 1. Headline

Detector v1 was right on 55 of its 66 firings (precision 0.833, agent-labelled)
over 3,901 opportunity records: exact on NIH activity codes, wrong on per-PI and
per-project limits, as expected. With the five misfiring rules removed (v2), every
one of its 48 firings on the same snapshot is labelled correct — a figure biased
by construction, so departments switch on with v2 and C-028 stays marked as
measured on one agent-labelled snapshot.

## 4. Interpretation

The activity-code rule (T32, T34, T35, R25, P30, P50, U54, S10) carries most of
the department-scale programmes and was right every time. Free text needs the
noun the limit attaches to: "no more than three submissions" means nothing until
it says per institution, per PI or per project. v2 keeps only rules whose phrasing
names the institution or the unit ("each university may nominate", "institutional
submission limits", "nominated by the university", "limited submission").
Decision per the protocol: every reason's v2 precision is ≥ 0.90 with at least one
correct row, so all three reasons ship. Supports C-028 as scoped there.

## 5. Threats to validity

- Labels by the implementing agent, not a person (ISSUES.md).
- v2 is measured on the rows that motivated its removals; a new snapshot is the
  real test.
- Recall unmeasured; the department channel will miss calls phrased outside the
  kept rules.
- One corpus snapshot, mostly US federal grants and US fellowships; EU calls state
  limits differently and barely appear among the firings.

## 6. What surprised us

The negation: "There is no limit on the number of applications" matched the
limit pattern. Any future limit rule needs to see the words before the match.

## 7. Next

Relabel by a person; re-measure v2 on the next collector snapshot (fresh
firings only); measure recall on a random sample of non-firing records.
