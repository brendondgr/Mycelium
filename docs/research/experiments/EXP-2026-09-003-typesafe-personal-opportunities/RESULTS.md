# EXP-2026-09-003 — Results

**Model:** TypeSafe `jev-1.13.0`, pinned. **Data:** one reader's grant and
conference slates on 2026-09-17, 57 (theme, item) pairs over three themes.
**Judgements:** none — both comparators are system outputs (PROTOCOL §Data).
**Runs:** 1 main pass; 5 pairs repeated 3× for determinism. Every number below
is read from `data/metrics.json`, written by `code/analyse.py` from
`data/pairs.json`.

## 1. The headline

TypeSafe's argmax fit level matched the prose model's stored verdict on
**56.1 %** of 57 pairs (pre-registered bar 50 %: **met**) and within one step on
**77.2 %** (bar 85 %: **missed**). Kendall tau between the fused slate position
and the `worth` probability averaged **+0.06** across six themes (range −0.24 to
+0.36) — below the 0.1–0.5 prior: on this reader's slates the fused order and
the decision model's worth are essentially uncorrelated. The model separated
every ten-item theme into **9.2 distinct fit scores on average**, against the
1.99 the LLM rubric managed in `EXP-2026-08-008`. The whole pass cost
**$0.0031** and took **4.0 s** of wall clock for 57 calls.

## 4. Interpretation

**Hypothesis 1 — half met.** Exact agreement clears its bar; within-one does
not, and the gap is concentrated: 13 pairs sit two or more steps apart
(`tables/disagreements.md`). In most of them TypeSafe is the harsher judge —
a stored `read` or `skim` that it puts at `skip`. Two of the fused **#1**
positions are among them: the grants theme *Agentic AI & Knowledge Graphs*
leads with an item the prose model called `read` and TypeSafe scores 0.67 on the
0–3 fit scale (`skip`, p(read) = 0.01); the conferences theme *AI for Healthcare
& Cybersecurity* leads with a generic symposium that both judges call `skim` but
TypeSafe ranks 7th of 10 by worth. Which judge is right is exactly what the
reader can now settle by looking, and nothing in this record can.

**Hypothesis 2 — below the prior.** The fused order carries almost no
information about worth as this model sees it. That is consistent with what the
record already says about the fused score (C-004 unsupported; the verdict
"explains an item's place, it never decides it") and it is the reason the
tables show two orders side by side rather than one.

**The C-016 mechanism is gone.** Nine distinct scores in ten rows, from a
probability-weighted mean, against two distinct values from an emitted integer.
Whether the *ordering* those scores give is better than the fused one needs
relevance labels — the LongEval experiment that follows this one — but the
specific failure that refuted pointwise LLM scoring does not recur here.

**Hypothesis 3 — between the bar and the falsifier.** `worth` moved by at most
0.03 across repeats (bar 0.01, falsifier 0.05), so single-run numbers stand,
with that caveat. The fit *score* moved by up to 0.13 on a 0–3 scale, about a
twentieth of the range. The provider documents no determinism guarantee, and
neither did this record assume one. A per-pair verdict near a level boundary
can flip between runs; the tables should be read with that in mind.

**Confidence is low on average.** Mean fit confidence 0.49. Reading the tables:
confidence is high (≥ 0.7) exactly where both judges say `read` and where both
say `skip`, and low in the middle, which is what a calibrated distribution
should do on a four-level scale where `file` and `skim` overlap. The product's
own docstring already admits `file` is the least distinct level.

**The exploratory questions.** Grant `eligible` is high (0.86–1.00) on the
off-topic EU calls and 0.2–0.4 on the topical US calls — plausibly a correct
reading of "any legal entity" versus institution-typed eligibility, but it is
unverified and should not be shown to anyone. Conference `venue_fit` tracks
`worth` closely and is highest (0.81–0.89) on the venues a reader in these
themes would expect.

**One retrieval finding, not a model finding.** In the *Local-First LLM
Systems* grants theme, eight of nine items are `skip` for both judges and
TypeSafe's mean worth is 0.13 — the slate for that theme is mostly defence,
ocean and circular-economy calls. Two independent judges agreeing that a theme's
slate is empty of anything worth reading says something about the grant pool
for that theme, not about either judge (follow-up below).

**C-026** — agreement ≥ half and more than two distinct scores per theme — is
**supported on this reader**, with the scope it was written with: agreement
between two system outputs, one account, 57 pairs, no relevance labels.

## 5. Threats to validity

- **No ground truth.** Agreement with the prose model is not correctness; the
  prose model's verdicts are themselves unmeasured (C-004). The tables let one
  reader judge, and Q-029's rule applies: that is a preference, never aggregated.
- **One reader, one day.** 57 pairs, three themes, one profile. Nothing here
  generalises to another account, and the protocol says why it must not be run
  on one without the data-processing terms read.
- **Shared inputs, different framings.** Both judges saw the same profile text
  and the same brief, but the prose model saw ten candidates at once with an
  evidence packet, and TypeSafe saw one candidate with a strand block. Some
  disagreement is the framing, not the model.
- **Level texts were written for this run.** The four fit descriptions extend
  the product's verdict blurbs; a different wording would move the boundaries.
- **Repeat spread.** See Hypothesis 3. Verdicts near a boundary are not stable
  to the second decimal.
- **Cost is list price** on the run date, in early access; it can change.

## 6. What surprised us

That the fused order and worth are *uncorrelated* rather than weakly aligned,
and that the disagreement lands on two fused #1 positions. And the speed: 57
judgements in four seconds at concurrency eight, median 195 ms each — the whole
grant-and-conference slate for a reader in less time than one prose-model call
takes on the self-hosted endpoint.

## 7. Follow-ups

Copied into `../../OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

- Q-038 — is the fit score's repeat spread (0.13 on 0–3) noise that averages
  out over three calls, or does it move verdicts near a boundary systematically?
  Cheap: repeat all 57 pairs three times (~$0.01).
- Q-039 — the *Local-First LLM Systems* grants slate is `skip` for both judges
  on eight of nine items. Is the grant pool for that theme genuinely empty, or is
  retrieval surfacing the wrong region of it? Answerable from the reachability
  measure in `EXP-2026-08-013` with the theme's interests as the query.

  is the one that puts relevance labels behind any of this.
