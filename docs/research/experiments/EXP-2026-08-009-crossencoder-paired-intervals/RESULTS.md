# EXP-2026-08-009 — Results

**Question:** does the cross-encoder's advantage over BM25 survive a paired
confidence interval at a larger query set? (`Q-016`)

**Answer: no. C-013 is refuted.** The advantage does not survive at n = 100, and
at n = 492 the cross-encoder is significantly *worse* than the baseline it
reranks.

Tables are generated by `code/paired.py`; see [`tables/paired.md`](tables/paired.md).
10,000 paired bootstrap resamples over query ids, seed fixed.

## 1. The result

### `longeval-sci-2026/snapshot-1/train` — 100 queries, `dctr` qrels

| Contrast | mean Δ nDCG@10 | 95% CI | excludes 0 | win/loss/tie | sign p |
|---|---:|---|:--:|---|---:|
| xenc over bm25 d50 − bm25_only | +0.0001 | [−0.0418, +0.0427] | no | 41/37/22 | 0.73 |
| xenc over bm25 d100 − bm25_only | +0.0087 | [−0.0342, +0.0525] | no | 43/36/21 | 0.50 |
| xenc over fusion d50 − bm25_only | +0.0088 | [−0.0349, +0.0537] | no | 41/39/20 | 0.91 |
| xenc over fusion d50 − fusion_union_pool | +0.0243 | [−0.0104, +0.0609] | no | 44/33/23 | 0.25 |
| xenc over fusion d100 − fusion_union_pool | +0.0249 | [−0.0097, +0.0614] | no | 45/33/22 | 0.21 |
| fusion_union_pool − bm25_only | −0.0156 | [−0.0526, +0.0190] | no | 38/42/20 | 0.74 |

**Not one contrast excludes zero.** Every sign test is non-significant. This
includes the two that `EXP-2026-08-008` reported as findings: the cross-encoder
beating BM25 (+0.0088, p = 0.91 — the least significant contrast in the table) and
the cross-encoder beating its own fusion base (+0.0243, p = 0.25).

The win/loss records explain why. +0.0088 comes from 41 queries better and 39
worse — a coin flip whose mean is decided by the size of a few wins. That is the
same pattern `EXP-2026-08-008` §2 identified for the LLM arm and correctly called
"no signal"; the cross-encoder was given a pass on it only because nobody had
computed the interval.

### `longeval-sci/2025-01` — 492 queries, released `test` qrels

| Contrast | mean Δ nDCG@10 | 95% CI | excludes 0 | win/loss/tie | sign p |
|---|---:|---|:--:|---|---:|
| xenc over bm25 d50 − bm25_only | **−0.0439** | [−0.0597, −0.0283] | **yes** | 139/218/135 | 3.4e−05 |
| xenc over bm25 d100 − bm25_only | **−0.0541** | [−0.0704, −0.0380] | **yes** | 130/228/134 | 2.5e−07 |

At the sample size that actually has power, the cross-encoder is **significantly
harmful**, on the mean and on the per-query record, and it gets worse with depth
— reranking 100 candidates hurts more than reranking 50. It loses on 218 queries
and wins on 139.

Absolute figures: `bm25_only` 0.2486 → 0.2047 at depth 50 → 0.1944 at depth 100.

## 2. Where the harm is, and where it is not

Per-query Δ for `xenc over bm25 d100` − `bm25_only` on `sci-2025-01`, by query
length:

| query length | queries | mean Δ nDCG@10 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 word | 231 | −0.0530 |
| 2 words | 181 | −0.0510 |
| 3–4 words | 48 | −0.1081 |
| **5+ words** | **32** | **+0.0009** |

The harm is concentrated on **short keyword queries** and vanishes on long ones.
This is consistent with the mechanism recorded in ISSUES.md §2: a cross-encoder is
trained on natural-language questions and has almost nothing to read in a one- or
two-word query, whereas BM25's rare-term exact match is at its best exactly there.
It also fits the cross-snapshot pattern — 2026's topics are longer and its result
is null rather than negative.

Read it carefully, though. The 3–4 word bucket is the **worst**, which breaks any
clean monotonic story, and the neutral bucket is the smallest (32 queries). The
same stratification on 2026 does not resolve at all (buckets of 5–35 queries, no
consistent direction). So "short queries are where it hurts" is **supported and
not explained**; the specific mechanism is not established.

**This is the finding with the most direct bearing on the product.** A declared
research interest in Mycelium is a short phrase — two or three words — which is
precisely the regime where the cross-encoder does the most damage here.

## 3. Two confounds that cannot be separated

`sci-2026-train` and `sci-2025-01` differ in judgment provenance (click-derived
`dctr` versus released `test` assessments) *and* in query style. A cross-encoder
trained on MS MARCO — itself click-derived — may simply agree more with
click-derived judgments. Either explanation produces the observed pattern, and
this experiment cannot distinguish them (ISSUES.md §2).

What survives regardless of which is right: **there is no snapshot in this record
where the cross-encoder significantly improves anything.**

## 4. What this changes

- **C-013 — refuted.** "A cross-encoder reranking the fusion ranking beats BM25
  alone" fails its own pre-registered falsifier: the interval includes zero on the
  snapshot it was measured on, and the closest testable variant is significantly
  negative on the snapshot with power.
- **C-010 stays refuted, and now more firmly.** The deployed stack does not beat a
  strong lexical baseline, and neither does any reranker tried on top of it.
- **C-014 (SPLADE raises recall as a third channel) is untouched.** It is a recall
  claim; nothing here re-tests R@100, and the recall gaps in `EXP-2026-08-008`
  (+0.063) are an order of magnitude larger than the nDCG@10 effects that failed
  to resolve. It should still be re-tested with intervals before being relied on.
- **The `EXP-2026-08-008` recommendation to adopt a cross-encoder is withdrawn.**
- **Every other contrast in `EXP-2026-08-008` inherits the warning.** That
  experiment reported eleven nDCG@10 differences at n = 100 without intervals. The
  six re-tested here all span zero. Differences of the size it discussed —
  ±0.01 to ±0.02 — are simply not resolvable at that sample size, and the ones it
  leaned on hardest (SPLADE's −0.060 deficit, the LLM's −0.057 order gap) are the
  only ones comfortably outside that band.

## 5. Threats to validity

1. **Only the BM25-based arms ran on `sci-2025-01`.** The fusion-based arms need a
   ~2 h corpus encode and were not run, so C-013's exact wording is refuted on
   2026 (interval spans zero) and by extension from the BM25 variant on 2025-01,
   not by a direct measurement of that contrast on 2025-01 (ISSUES.md §3).
2. **Two confounds between snapshots**, above.
3. **Pooled scope, re-ranking evaluation** on both snapshots.
4. **Wrong task.** LongEval is `query → documents`; Mycelium is
   `declared-interest profile → feed`. `Q-001` is untouched.
5. **One cross-encoder.** `Xenova/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`, a 22M-parameter model
   chosen because this host has no GPU. A larger reranker (`bge-reranker-base`) may
   behave differently, particularly on short queries — see `Q-021`.

## 6. What to do next

1. **Do not adopt the cross-encoder** on this evidence. The honest summary is that
   it is null at best and harmful on short queries.
2. **Re-test C-014 and C-008 with paired intervals.** Both are recall claims with
   much larger effects, and they are now the only ladder findings still standing.
   Recall metrics need the same treatment nDCG@10 just received.
3. **`Q-021` — try a stronger reranker on short queries** before concluding that
   cross-encoders as a family do not fit this task. One 22M model on a CPU is a
   thin basis for a family-level conclusion.
4. **Every future contrast in this record ships with an interval.** The design
   lesson is bigger than the arm: at n = 100 this collection cannot resolve
   anything smaller than roughly ±0.04 nDCG@10, which is wider than most of the
   differences the project has been discussing.
