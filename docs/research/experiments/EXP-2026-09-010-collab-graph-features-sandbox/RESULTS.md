# EXP-2026-09-010 — Results

## 1. Headline

On the 600-person world with 400 planted cross-lab co-authorships, every
graph feature named only whom it may (H1: **0 violations** — the co-authorship
table matched the plan exactly; every introduction path was a real chain of
co-authors through people open to the seeker, and the shortest; nobody within
two co-authorships appeared "outside my network"; every team member passed
the gate; every search at country and anywhere scope stayed inside audience ∩
scope). H2 **failed as registered** (team cover precision 0.742 < 0.80); with
covers certified by concepts only it is **0.995** (post hoc).

## 4. Interpretation

- **Consent survives the graph.** No introduction named anyone who had not
  opted in to the seeker; when the only chain ran through someone who had not,
  the answer was "no path", and the brute-force reference agreed every time.
- **Paths are rare in a world like this**: 6.6% of people shown had one within
  three co-authorships through consenting people. With 30% of people open and
  sparse cross-lab ties that is expected; real co-authorship is denser.
- **A team needs stronger evidence than a ranking.** Sentence matches rank
  people well enough in a lane, where only the strongest few are shown, but as
  proof that someone *has* a method they were wrong 13 times in 14 (ISSUES 2);
  certifying covers by concept only lifted precision to 0.995 and teams grew
  from 1.6 to 2.1 people, which is what needing three or four methods from
  other fields should look like.
- **Wider scopes are sound in the sandbox.** Country and anywhere searches
  never leaked outside audience ∩ scope. Whether to widen live is a separate,
  evidence-gated decision (plan CN-08's readiness check, EXP-2026-09-012).

## 5. Threats to validity

- Cross-lab co-authorship is planted uniformly at random (400 two-author
  works), not as real collaboration networks form.
- Team precision is judged on planted method names; a person holding an
  unplanted synonym would count as wrong.
- The rerank was off (ISSUES / PROTOCOL); run 2 is post hoc (ISSUES 2).

## 6. What surprised us

How confidently a sentence can "match" the wrong method: "random forest"
against a sentence about a hierarchical Bayesian model.
