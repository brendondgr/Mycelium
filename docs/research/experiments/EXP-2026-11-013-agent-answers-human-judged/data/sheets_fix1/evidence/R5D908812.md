Eligibility and Selection Criteria DDFs are intended as recruitment fellowships. To that end, only those students who are seeking admission to a doctoral program at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (Illinois) are eligible. Generally, this includes students at either one of two stages of study: students seeking first-time admission to a doctoral program, or students who have completed a master’s at Illinois and are now applying for admission to a doctoral program here at Illinois. Only those students intending to enter during the 2027-28 academic year are eligible. For students intending to enter in Spring 2028, their fellowship will not begin until Fall 2028. Full-time Illinois employees are ineligible. Students who have previously received a Graduate College Equity Master's Fellowship (EMF) or an Aspire Master's Fellowship remain eligible for consideration for the Dean’s Fellowship, provided they meet all other eligibility requirements. Candidates must be U.S. citizens, permanent residents, or approved domestic applicants . (If the student is tagged in Slate as "domestic," the student meets this criterion.) The Dean’s Doctoral Fellowship is open to newly admitted students in an Illinois doctoral degree program who: Demonstrate academic achievement and potential for success in graduate study (as assessed by the nominating department, transcript, honors, or other designation); and Have experienced financial hardship, are first generation in their families to attend college, experienced barriers to educational access, or followed a non-traditional path to graduate education; and Have demonstrated their ability to contribute to a community characterized by inclusion, belonging, respect, and productive collaboration. Candidates should provide an example of how they have engaged with students, colleagues, or communities with different perspectives, abilities, and experiences to achieve a positive outcome and reflect on what they learned from the experience. Award Structure – Two Versions: The DDF pays $75,000 in total stipend over the duration of the fellowship. However, there are two versions of the fellowship, each with its own payment structure. At the time of nomination, the department must select which version to offer the student. If awarded, students may not switch to the other version. Version 1: $25,000 annual stipend for three years, plus a full tuition waiver. This option is primarily for departments that intend for the fellowship to serve as the student’s main source of funding for the three “on fellowship” years. ### Evidence #20 [paper] The need for, feasibility of, and barriers to implementation of a trauma traveling fellowship: a survey of ESTES members
Authors: Jared M. Wohlgemut, S.C. Gupta, Stefano P. B. Cioffi, Heura Llaquet Bayo, Helen S. Wohlgemut, Jonathan Hobby, Shahin Mohseni, Diego Mariani
Venue: European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00068-026-03321-2
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Abstract Purpose Many European surgeons lack access to high-volume trauma training. A Trauma Traveling Fellowship, supported by the European Society of Trauma and Emergency Surgery (ESTES), could broaden educational and clinical exposure to trauma care, but feasibility and implementation factors are unclear. The aims of this survey are to assess the aspirations, expectations, and perceived challenges of prospective fellows and host centres for an ESTES Trauma Traveling Fellowship. Methods A cross-sectional, web-based survey was distributed to all ESTES members, capturing demographics, fellowship aspirations, perceived barriers, and host centre capacities. The survey incorporated Likert scales, free-text responses, and was guided by the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) and the Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance / Practical Robust Implementation and Sustainability Model (RE-AIM/PRISM). Results Of 903 invited, 210 responses (23.3%) were analysed. The median age was 38 years; 71% were consultants, and 29% were residents. Most (71%) would consider applying for a Fellowship, and 47% stated their centre could host Fellows. Only 23% currently access funded fellowships. Key aspirations included hands-on participation in trauma care, although hosts could more often only guarantee observational roles. Major barriers included funding and institutional permissions. Most respondents supported implementing the fellowship as it was perceived to align with ESTES priorities. Conclusions There is strong support among European trauma surgeons for an ESTES Trauma Traveling Fellowship, recognising both its potential value and practical impediments. Addressing funding and regulatory barriers will be crucial. A structured and adaptable implementation approach, combined with ongoing evaluation, may help ensure long-term success.
