## Question

Which open grant in our feed fits the Marine heatwave early warning project best?

## Answer

Based on the current feed for the **Marine heatwave early warning** project (#1), the best fit among the open grants is **#6 Unlocking a safe operating space for Antarctica and the Southern Ocean**.

**Why it fits:**
The grant specifically calls for improving projections of the future of the Southern Ocean, including the study of "extreme events and mid-latitude weather" (#6), which aligns with the project's focus on marine heatwave forecasting (#1).

**Biggest doubt:**
The grant is strictly limited to the Antarctic and Southern Ocean region (#6), whereas the project's current scope is for the "regional coast" (#1) without a specified location.

**Other options in the feed:**
*   **#4 A stronger Global Ocean Observing System...**: This is a very large-scale infrastructure grant focused on the deployment of observation equipment and governance frameworks, which is less aligned with the project's specific focus on ML-corrected forecasting and dashboards.
*   **#3, #5, #7-12**: These cover unrelated areas such as carbon sinks, marine geology, and sustainable tourism.

# Judging a call's fit

1. Read the call with `corpus_item` (deadlines, eligibility, amount, whether it is
   still open). If it is closed, say so first.
2. Read who it is for: `projects_get` for a project, `org_lab` for a lab,
   `me_profile` / `me_interests` for the person.
3. Compare concretely: the call's aims against the project's problem and
   method; its eligibility rules (career stage, citizenship, institution type)
   against what you know; its deadline against the project's milestones.
4. Say what fits, what does not, and what you could not check (eligibility you
   cannot see, a budget you do not know). Never guess eligibility.
5. If the question is who should apply, read the `who-should-apply` skill.

Honesty rules, as in Mylo's reports: no scores or percentages, no hype, no
"perfect fit". One plain sentence on the strongest reason and one on the
biggest doubt. Cite the call and the project by #n.
