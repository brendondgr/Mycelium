### Evidence #2 [paper] Global stakeholder preferences for point-of-care HIV nucleic acid amplification tests: A conjoint study
Authors: Kara M. Palamountain, Chethana Achar, Rebecca P Kirby, Yukari Carol Manabe, Debi Boeras, Michal Maimaran
Venue: PLoS ONE
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0361025
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Introduction This study aimed to assess global stakeholder preferences for key attributes of point-of-care (POC) HIV Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAAT), with the aim of informing product development to support progress toward UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets. Methods A web-based conjoint survey was conducted in August through November 2024 among HIV clinicians, laboratory professionals, and researchers. Participants were asked to evaluate hypothetical POC HIV NAAT profiles varying by six attributes: sensitivity / limit of detection (LOD), sample-collection method, turnaround time (TAT), result format, price, and power source. Relative importance and part-worth utilities were calculated to assess trade-offs in decision-making. Results One hundred three respondents from 33 countries, primarily in Africa (75%), completed the survey. The relative importance placed on the attributes in the conjoint task varied based on the setting. In community and home-based settings, participants prioritized fingerstick sample collection (25.90%) and faster TATs (28.8%), even at the expense of sensitivity (15.70%). By contrast, respondents from hospitals and health centers valued tests with high sensitivity (42.6%, 40.9%) and faster TATs (31.5%, 22.6%). Across all respondents, price (7.0%), result format (3.30%), and power source (7.25%) were less influential. Conclusions Stakeholders were willing to make trade-offs among desirable attributes, prioritizing rapid turnaround time, strong sensitivity, and fingerstick sampling over affordability, power requirements, and result format in the conjoint task. These preferences vary by level of care and by use case, underscoring the importance of aligning product features with contextual needs. Engaging stakeholders early in the design process is critical to ensure new POC HIV NAAT technologies are both acceptable and scalable in low-resource settings.

Abstract:

This paper presents the design, regulatory compliance mapping, and pilot deployment of the Laboratory Tracking Software (LTS) platform, a cloud-ready, web-based system that unifies laboratory information management system (LIMS) and quality management system (QMS) functions under a risk-based Computer Software Assurance (CSA) framework aligned with U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 21 CFR Part 11 and ISO/IEC 17025. ### Evidence #4 [paper] Selecting Materials Outreach Topics from Community Repair Demand: Organizational Weighting, Temporal Transfer and Local Fit
Authors: Chahn Yong Jung
Venue: Journal of Materials Education (Electronic)
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.71448/jme2026346
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Community repair events bring materials science into contact with familiar products, but outreach teams must decide which objects deserve limited preparation time. This study evaluates topic selection under two representation rules: equal influence for each recorded repair attempt and equal influence for each repair group. The Open Repair Alliance collection contains 48,669 attempts. Selection used 17,805 attempts from 95 groups, followed by evaluation on 18,802 attempts from 126 groups. The four topics selected by attempt weighting were laptops, lamps, separate audio equipment and coffee makers. Group weighting replaced the latter two with mobile phones and small kitchen appliances. Its selection-period advantage in average group coverage contracted from 3.09 to 0.43 percentage points in the following year. Network-stratified group resampling gave a 95% interval of −1.29 to 2.28 points for that contrast, while attempt coverage decreased by 3.27 points. Local differences had opposing signs: 58 groups gained coverage, 63 lost coverage and five were unchanged. Resampling the selection groups recovered the complete topic sets in only 19.28% and 35.84% of draws. Increasing the kit to eight topics made the two rules select identical categories; ten topics were needed to exceed one-half of average group demand. These results establish the consequences of representation choices without attributing learning gains to category matching. A shared materials kit therefore requires an explicit intended beneficiary, host-specific preparation and an educational assessment independent of repair completion.

Authors: Zexiang Dai, Wentao Ye, Jin Zhou, Lei Tian
Venue: Frontiers in Public Health
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1896681
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Aim This study aimed to systematically review model-based economic evaluations of next-generation sequencing for guiding targeted therapy in non-small cell lung cancer. Methods We searched PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, VIP Database, and Wanfang Database from inception to March 20, 2026, to identify economic evaluations of next-generation sequencing for molecular testing in non-small cell lung cancer. Future evaluations should therefore assess NGS within the broader molecular testing–treatment pathway and better reflect real-world clinical practice.

Abstract:
The United Kingdom government's 10 Year Health Plan for England sets out three shifts for a future-ready health service in England: shifting care from hospitals to communities, harnessing technology in health and care, and prioritising prevention over treatment. In this spirit, a large eye-hospital network in central London piloted a community-based, technician-led, asynchronous/virtual-review clinic to monitor stable chronic eye conditions and reduce avoidable harms, a model which could supplement traditional clinician-led services. ### Evidence #9 [paper] The clinical utility of carrier screening.
Authors: Gruzin, Mia J, Burnett, Leslie
Venue: NPJ genomic medicine
Year: 2026
Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42547507/
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Carrier screening aims to identify couples at risk of having children with serious genetic disorders. However, screening panels vary markedly in size, gene content and price, with little guidance to assess clinical utility or value. We analysed 89 carrier screening panels from 30 global providers, modelling test performance using pathogenic variants from gnomAD v4.1.0 and ClinVar across ten ancestry groups and two synthetic populations (representing the United States and Australia). Year: 2026

Abstract:
BACKGROUND: Rising skin cancer incidence increases pressure on the healthcare system. Artificial intelligence (AI)-based mobile health applications (mHealth apps) are being offered to laypersons from the general population to be used on their own initiative. It is unclear if, and how much, they are willing to pay (WTP) for these apps and how they decide on their valuation.
