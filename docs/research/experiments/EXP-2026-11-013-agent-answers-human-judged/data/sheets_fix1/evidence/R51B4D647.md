Abstract:

### Evidence #3 [paper] A Systematic Survey of Molecular Taggants, Distributed Ledger Technology, and Machine Learning for Verifiable Plastic Waste Traceability
Authors: Purva Shinde, Adweta Dengale, Abhiraj Gatmane, Nayan Rathod, Umesh Pawar
Venue: Zenodo (CERN European Organization for Nuclear Research)
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23610052
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is now the dominant regulatory instrument for plastic packaging in India, the European Union and elsewhere. It rests on an assumption that current systems cannot test: that a producer's declaration of recovered and recycled tonnage is true. Compliance evidence is aggregate, self-reported and detached from the material it describes, so the central claim of the regime is cheap to falsify and hard to disprove. This paper is a systematic survey conducted under a PRISMA-style protocol. We screened 312 records from five databases and retained 45 sources published between 2023 and 2026 across four communities that seldom cite one another: molecular marking of polymers, machine-readable identifiers, distributed ledger technology (DLT) for provenance, and machine learning for waste classification, forecasting and anomaly detection. The included studies are quality-appraised, and the four bodies of work are compared against one another using a four-layer analytical framework and six capability criteria derived from the regulatory requirements. Three principal findings emerge. Not one reviewed configuration satisfies all six criteria, and the sharpest — whether an identifier survives mechanical reprocessing — is failed categorically by every approach built on digital identifiers alone. Where one layer is weak another is characteristically strong, yet no reviewed study evaluates the layers in composition. The third finding carries furthest: because no shared dataset, protocol or benchmark permits reported accuracies and throughputs to be compared, the field's apparent maturity cannot be verified, and its limiting constraint proves evaluative rather than technical. We specify what an adequate benchmark requires, and derive a reference framework whose design decisions are each traced to supporting evidence. Implementation and empirical validation are outside the scope of this survey.

### Evidence #6 [paper] PlasticFADE: a predictive model for plastic fragmentation and degradation rates in the environment
Authors: Yibo Tang, Nadim Saadi, Jérôme Lavoie, Anne‐Marie Boulay
Venue: Microplastics and Nanoplastics
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1186/s43591-026-00243-z
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Abstract Plastics are widespread across the globe, posing risks to environmental and human health. Fragmentation and degradation are key processes that govern the environmental fate and impacts of plastics, yet their rates remain poorly quantified. Life cycle assessment (LCA) is widely recognized as the standard framework for comprehensively evaluating the environmental performance of products and services. Accurately capturing the fate of plastics in natural environments therefore represents a critical priority for impact assessment modeling. However, the degradation and fragmentation mechanisms governing plastic polymer behavior remain poorly quantified in current life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) models, limiting their capacity to characterize the potential biodiversity impacts arising from plastic emissions to natural environments. In this study, experimental data on plastic fragmentation and degradation were systematically compiled from the literature. Using these data, PlasticFADE was developed as the first iteration of an empirical model to predict polymer-specific rate constants in various environmental compartments. The model predictions reveal that the rate constants vary considerably across polymers and compartments. Estimated lifespans of plastics range from several decades to over a millennium. Beaches are identified as hotspots for both fragmentation and degradation, whereas plastics are most likely to remain intact in the water column. Criteria are proposed for evaluating the reliability of the model outputs, which should be interpreted with due caution. Despite its limitations, the model offers valuable insights into plastic fate processes and offers significant potential for improving current LCIA methods for plastic emissions assessment.

Abstract:
Bioplastics have emerged as promising alternatives to conventional fossil-based plastics because of their potential to reduce dependence on non-renewable resources and support the transition towards a circular economy. However, their environmental sustainability is determined not only by feedstock selection but also by polymer characteristics, product design, and the effectiveness of end-of-life management systems. Venue: Data
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.3390/data11100266
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Sparse and heterogeneous plastic-related data complicate the construction of analytical datasets and the evaluation of predictive models. This study presents AUDA, a workflow that organizes human-reviewed records extracted by PRISM into country–year analytical tables and supports descriptive analysis, interpolation, and forecasting. The analytical database contains over 28,000 records from 195 countries and regions. The case studies use smaller datasets constructed from available observations, along with an external global plastic-production series. Correlation and random-forest feature-importance analyses describe associations with plastic waste generation. Predictive evaluations compare interpolation, statistical, and machine-learning methods using repeated masking and rolling-origin forecasting. Cubic spline interpolation and Gaussian process regression achieved the lowest mean weighted absolute percentage error in the Japan and United States resin-consumption interpolation tasks, respectively. Theil–Sen regression achieved the lowest error using this metric in global plastic-production forecasting, while persistence and ARIMA tied for the lowest error in forecasting Japanese plastic-waste generation. These rankings are specific to the evaluated series and data partitions. An exploratory sensitivity analysis also examines how a standard-error tolerance coefficient and the chosen complexity ordering affect hyperparameter selection; it does not establish improved performance on unseen data. AUDA provides workflows for analyzing available records but does not reconcile differences in source measurement methods or estimate policy effects.

Abstract:
Microbial-mediated plastic degradation has the potential to address the persistent global problems of plastic waste and pollution. Previous work has shown that soils can harbour microbes capable of plastic degradation, but we expect there is a broader diversity of soil microbes capable of metabolizing plastics than identified to date using more traditional cultivation-based screening methods. Here we demonstrate a novel approach to identify putative plastic degrading microbes in soil. We paired in situ, real-time measurements of microbial plastic degradation on conductive sensors with subsequent microbial community profiling of the sensor-associated biofilms exhibiting appreciable degradation. Abstract:
Marine plastic pollution threatens ecosystems, economies, and human health, yet economic literature overlooks the transport of plastic debris across marine ecosystem compartments and its natural breakdown. Here we show, using a system dynamics model, the global physical and economic efforts required to remove buoyant marine plastic debris by 2050. The model simulates the degradation, transport, and cleanup of macroplastics and their breakdown into microplastics across shoreline, coastal, and offshore zones. Abstract The manufacturing industry faces the challenge of using resources more efficiently and implementing circular practices. A promising pathway is to establish a circular economy (CE) through additive manufacturing (AM), enabling on-demand production with low material input and allowing the use of recycled materials. Despite this potential, practical barriers hinder its adoption and are insufficiently understood from a multi-stakeholder perspective. This paper combines stakeholder theory with the circular supply chain management concept in a qualitative multiple-case study to examine the perspectives and interactions of the actors involved. The findings separate the general technical adoption barriers of AM from the socioeconomic and relational barriers of circularity, which arise from power asymmetries, information asymmetries, and coordination failures among interdependent actors of differing salience. These barriers vary substantially with firms’ initial conditions, including their AM technology maturity, their engagement in closed-loop practices, and the managerial relevance they assign to circularity. The study develops five propositions based on the cross-case analysis and identifies three distinct pathways from barrier to solution for firms at different stages of circular supply chain development. Trust-based partnerships between manufacturing and recycling companies can close material loops locally, but they remain fragile amid price competition from virgin materials. Durable circularity therefore requires governance safeguards, including guaranteed-offtake contracts, verifiable production information, and consolidated reverse logistics, all supported by regulatory intervention. The study contributes to the CE and operations management literature by providing a deeper understanding of these barriers and proposing actionable measures for stakeholders to promote circularity in the specific AM domain.

### Evidence #20 [paper] Review Performance of New Active Substances in Taiwan: A 10-Year Retrospective Analysis (2015–2024)
Authors: Chun-Yi Huang, Ling-Kuei Chen, Bi-Kai Hsu, Hwei‐Fang Cheng, Chung‐Hsuen Wu

Year: 2026

Abstract:
Abstract Background Since 2011, the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration (TFDA) has established various facilitated regulatory pathways (FRPs, including expedited and accelerated review frameworks) for new drug applications. In 2017 and 2019, TFDA implemented the Refuse to File (RTF) mechanism and conducted a comprehensive revision of FRPs, respectively. This study aims to evaluate the outcomes of these reforms from 2015 to 2024, focusing on New Active Substance (NAS) approvals, pathway utilization, and international comparison. Methods NAS approvals between 2015 and 2024 were analyzed using TFDA’s internal regulatory database, with U.S. Food and Drug Administration (US FDA), European Medicines Agency (EMA), and Japan’s Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (Japan’s PMDA) data obtained from public sources. Analyses and visualizations were conducted with R. Results Following the 2017 and 2019 reforms, the on-time rate for the 360-day standard pathway surged from 19.0% of 21 cases in 2015 to 100% in 16 cases in 2024. A distinct structural shift toward FRPs was observed, as the proportion of standard applications declined from 80.4% to 56.4%. A contextual comparison of median approval times indicated that TFDA’s timelines were broadly comparable to those of the EMA and moved closer to those of the US FDA and Japan’s PMDA over the study period. Conclusions The TFDA review timelines became substantially more predictable, and the pathway distribution shifted toward FRPs. These changes followed the 2017 and 2019 reforms and reflect their combined contribution rather than any single mechanism. The cross-agency figures reported here serve as a general point of reference rather than a direct efficiency comparison, given differences in product mix, therapeutic areas, pathway eligibility criteria, submission timing and clock-stop mechanisms. Building on these developments, the forthcoming reliance pilot program is expected to further support patient access and regulatory resilience.
