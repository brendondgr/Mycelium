Method (Mycelium's reading): A proactive, pre-emptive rescue strategy triggered by climate forecasts and expert workshop assessment, involving the temporary capture of wild individuals, holding them in captivity for three months, and releasing them after the heatwave subsided.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): Wild population of red handfish in Australia during the 2023/2024 El Niño event, specifically January 2024, with in situ water temperatures exceeding records.
Abstract:
Abstract As climate change drives increasingly frequent and severe climatic extremes, effective strategies to prevent extinction of threatened species are urgently needed. Interventions enabling species to survive acute stress may buy time to address other threats and identify long‐term refugia. For the Critically Endangered red handfish ( Thymichthys politus ), forecasts ahead of the 2023/2024 El Niño indicated a high risk of marine heatwaves (MHW). An expert workshop identified these forecasted extremes (>20 or +~3°C) as a severe threat, triggering an emergency rescue to temporarily remove individuals from the wild. Following commonwealth, state and institutional approvals, 25 fish were captured in January 2024 and held in captivity for 3 months. During this period, in situ water temperatures exceeded records, including 80 days more than 2°C above the long‐term mean summer temperature (~17.5°C). This represents Australia's first pre‐emptive rescue of a protected marine species and highlights the importance of proactive, adaptive conservation to strengthen capacity in safeguarding threatened species during environmental extremes and long term climate change impacts.

Abstract:
Heat early warning systems (HEWS) are critical tools for anticipating hazardous heat and enabling timely response. While some of the earliest systems were developed over two decades ago, little is known about how and why systems have evolved in practice. This study therefore examines the evolution of the Dutch heat warning system from 2007 to 2025 and evaluates the future performance of current warning criteria. We analysed 18 operational documents, including seven post-event evaluations, to identify operational challenges and drivers of change. Current warning criteria were also applied to KNMI’23 climate scenarios to assess future warning frequency, duration, intensity, and season length. Using Community Earth System Model version 2 simulations, ocean-assimilated experiments and five-year hindcasts initialized annually from 1981 to 2020, we show that frequency and cumulative intensity are predictable for several months to multiple years across many ocean basins, with skill varying strongly by region and season. Abstract:
The PROMOTE Sandpit brought together 54 participants from across ARIA’s Forecasting Tipping Points (FTP) programme to discuss development of a future Early Warning System (EWS) for tipping of North Atlantic Subpolar Gyre (SPG) and Greenland Ice sheet systems. The sandpit marks a significant shift from a focus on detecting climate tipping points towards designing actionable warning systems that support real-world decision making. ### Evidence #20 [paper] OCEAN-RAD P1: Conceptual Design, Numerical Modelling and Test Protocol for Radiative Cooling of Enclosed Seawater Tanks (v1.1)

Abstract:
OCEAN-RAD P1 is a preliminary engineering study investigating radiative sky cooling as a potential means of removing heat from enclosed seawater tanks. The proposed laboratory configuration comprises 2 m² of radiating panels, a closed freshwater circulation loop and a titanium heat exchanger coupled to a 100-litre seawater tank. Initial testing is intended to take place on land, without organisms, primarily under nocturnal conditions. The study provides a thermal model, reproducible Python code, synthetic parameter studies, a proposed experimental protocol, measurement templates and an internal technical review. A second numerical implementation reproduces the steady-state results across 1,008 prescribed parameter combinations. This agreement demonstrates computational consistency under shared assumptions; it does not establish experimental validity. Under one prescribed favourable nocturnal scenario, the model predicts an initial heat extraction rate of approximately 78 W. Performance depends on atmospheric radiation, temperatures, heat-transfer coefficients and component properties. The reported values are conditional numerical results rather than measured or representative coastal performance. Version 1.1 distinguishes heat transported between the heat-exchanger ports from heat extracted from the seawater, accounting for changes in the exchanger’s stored thermal energy during transients. It also strengthens data validation, extends uncertainty accounting and revises the proposed startup procedure. No prototype testing or biological experiments have been conducted. Practical efficiency, durability, economic viability and ecological benefits remain unverified. The work is intended to support laboratory evaluation and possible future applications in small, controlled marine facilities; it does not demonstrate cooling of the open ocean or a net planetary cooling effect. AI tools were used for technical drafting, numerical implementation and internal review. The internal review used the same AI system and does not constitute independent external peer review.
