### Evidence #2 [paper] Marine heatwave exposure is changing unevenly across Scottish aquaculture sites
Authors: Camila Toledo-Jaime, Jennifer A. Graham, Ben Hudson, David Andrew Moffat, Bryony L. Townhill, Steven J. Palmer
Venue: Frontiers in Marine Science
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2026.1963854
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Introduction Marine heatwaves (MHWs) are an increasing climate hazard for coastal aquaculture, yet regional assessments may not resolve how exposure varies across the locations where farms operate. Methods Here, we analysed daily ~5-km OSTIA sea surface temperature data from 1990 to 2025 at the locations of 361 active Scottish finfish and shellfish farms, represented by 218 unique grid cells, and compared satellite temperatures with in situ records from eight farms. We used principal component analysis and hierarchical clustering to describe long-term MHW exposure, and mixed-effects models to assess temporal changes. Results Two broad exposure profiles were identified. Cluster 1 was characterised by higher mean, maximum and cumulative intensity, whereas differences in event duration and frequency between clusters were smaller. Temporal trends, however, differed more strongly. MHW frequency increased at similar rates in both clusters, while event duration, cumulative intensity and severity increased more rapidly in Cluster 2. Between 1990 -2019 and 2020 -2025, estimated event duration increased by 44.6% in Cluster 1 and 89.9% in Cluster 2, while cumulative intensity increased by 50.0% and approximately 127.5%, respectively (cluster × period, p < 0.001). The estimated probability of a Severe or Extreme event rose from 2.2% to 26.2% in Cluster 2, compared with 1.8% to 6.1% in Cluster 1 (cluster × period, p < 0.001). Mean event intensity changed comparatively little. Discussion These findings show that MHW exposure across Scottish aquaculture is multidimensional, spatially uneven and changing at different rates through time. The resulting hazard -exposure landscape helps bridge regional MHW assessments and individual-farm monitoring, providing a foundation for more targeted monitoring and future assessment of aquaculture vulnerability and climate risk.

Abstract:
Accelerating marine heatwaves are outpacing static conservation strategies, threatening the functional integrity of temperate reefs globally. Implementing “climate-smart” Marine Spatial Planning requires moving beyond retrospective impact assessment toward prospective tools that can integrate local context to anticipate vulnerability. This study presents an explainable, policy-aware early warning system that leverages the most comprehensive historical record of mass mortality events (1986-2020) to forecast site-level risk in the Mediterranean coralligenous reefs. Using a rigorous modelling framework validated on independent prospective test data (2019-2020), the model achieves high forecasting skill (average precision = 0.75, where 1.0 is perfect and the baseline is 0.70) and maintains temporal robustness by simulating real-world forecasting across the historical record. Model interpretability indicates that macroclimatic thermal stress is modulated by local factors such as depth and spatial protection. Abstract:
Abstract. Storms and associated marine hazards pose a environmental risks, particularly under long-term changes driven by anthropogenic climate change. This study assesses the potential impacts of future climate change on annual median and extreme wave conditions in the North Sea and the Baltic Sea using a ensemble of up to 14 and 8 regional projections, respectively. The ensemble includes simulations based on IPCC CMIP3 and CMIP5 scenarios, incorporating a range of global and regional climate models, initial conditions, and emission scenarios to reflect diverse sources of uncertainty. The results indicate a robust increase in wave height in the eastern North Sea and many regions of the Baltic Sea, as well as a consistent decrease in the western North Sea, particularly under high-emission scenarios. However, these trends are superimposed on substantial temporal and internal variability, highlighting the importance of using ensemble-based assessments to evaluate future marine climate conditions.

Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) have emerged as an increasingly severe disturbance to global marine ecosystems, with documented intensifications in both frequency and magnitude over the past century. Western Australia has experienced some of the most severe MHWs on record, causing widespread ecological impacts, including coral bleaching and mortality, local species extinctions, and socioeconomic impacts such as fishery closures, making the region a critical case study of climate-driven change in the marine domain. Most recently, a severe MHW during summer 2024/2025 has caused widespread disruption to marine life, reinforcing the region's vulnerability to repeated extreme events. We emphasise that the successful implementation of such strategies hinges upon the accurate and timely prediction of future MHW events.

### Evidence #6 [paper] Framing and accuracy of AMOC reporting in Dutch journalistic media: comparing scientific research papers and news articles
Authors: Yente M. J. te Koppele, Erik van Sebille
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2026-6433
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Abstract. The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Current (AMOC) may be approaching a tipping point, with potentially significant environmental and societal consequences. Here, we examine the framing and accuracy of Dutch news articles reporting on the AMOC slowdown or potential collapse using peer-reviewed scientific research papers. In total, we analysed 17 scientific papers and 73 news articles published between 1999 and 2025 using qualitative coding, and eight scientists were interviewed to assess their opinion on the representation of their research in these news articles. Our analysis reveals that Dutch news articles often omit methodological detail and scientific uncertainties, while emphasizing the timing of an AMOC collapse. Dutch news articles simplify terminology, use types of exaggeration, and localize information to engage a broader public. Although most interviewed scientists consider the media coverage of their research adequate, they identify areas for improvement and propose solutions. Overall, Dutch journalistic media often accurately convey the core concepts of AMOC-related scientific research papers, but place strong emphasis on societal consequences and opportunities of an AMOC collapse.

### Evidence #7 [paper] Rethinking Marine Heatwaves: Towards a Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress
Authors: Natalija Dunić, Krešimir Ruić, Ivica Vilibić
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.22541/essoar.15011394/v1
Still open/current: yes
Problem (Mycelium's reading): Standard marine heatwave (MHW) definitions rely on statistical temperature anomalies relative to a climatological baseline, which does not directly account for the thermal exposure experienced by marine organisms or its biological consequences.
Method (Mycelium's reading): The Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress Wave (PMHSW) framework, which integrates temperature exceedance above a biological threshold across time and depth to estimate cumulative thermal exposure and relates this exposure to the probability of a defined biological response.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): Synthetic examples used to demonstrate the estimation of response functions from biological observations and the derivation of binary event definitions.
Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) are commonly defined as statistically extreme temperature events relative to a climatological baseline. While this approach provides a consistent description of ocean temperature extremes, it does not directly account for the thermal exposure experienced by marine organisms or its biological consequences. Here, we propose the Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress Wave (PMHSW) framework, which shifts the focus from temperature anomalies toward biologically relevant thermal exposure. The framework integrates temperature exceedance above a biological threshold across time and depth to estimate cumulative thermal exposure and relates this exposure to the probability of a defined biological response. The probabilistic formulation allows differences among organisms, habitats and thermal tolerances to be represented while retaining a continuous description of biological heat stress. Using synthetic examples, we demonstrate how the response function can be estimated from biological observations and how a binary event definition can be obtained when needed. The PMHSW framework is intended as a complementary approach to existing MHW definitions, particularly when the objective is to assess the potential biological consequences of marine thermal extremes under present and future climate conditions.

Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) negatively impact marine ecosystems and coastal economies. Here, we evaluate whether a preemptive, short-term, localized surface emission of sea salt aerosols (marine cloud brightening, MCB) can mitigate the impacts of a predicted MHW without creating significant effects beyond the intervention zone. ### Evidence #10 [paper] Is reliable climate change information ‘predictably uncertain’?
Authors: Matthias Ackermann
Venue: European Journal for Philosophy of Science
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-026-00757-6
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Abstract Recent discussion suggests that uncertainty in climate models’ representation of the interaction between external radiative forcing and internal variability prevents scientists from reliably providing local (or regional) climate change information anytime soon. In this paper, I articulate ‘predictable uncertainty’ as an epistemic criterion of reliable climate change information: the ability of climate scientists to systematically characterize and constrain the uncertainty envelope associated with their epistemic situation. There are two related implications. First, internal variability can be an informative signal for future climate change, and controlling for it can help identify the scales at which climate model-based or climate model-supported information may be reliable. Second, the reliability of local climate change information should be evaluated based not primarily on the amount of uncertainty in the interaction between the forced response and internal variability, but rather on climate scientists’ ability to characterize and constrain that uncertainty.

Abstract:
Heat early warning systems (HEWS) are critical tools for anticipating hazardous heat and enabling timely response. While some of the earliest systems were developed over two decades ago, little is known about how and why systems have evolved in practice. This study therefore examines the evolution of the Dutch heat warning system from 2007 to 2025 and evaluates the future performance of current warning criteria. Abstract:

Main results. With all four faces of a cell counted, the compression term is about one fifth of the conservative tendency magnitude near the peak (MHW 0.21, MCS 0.23) and the three-quarter “compression” of an internal-only graph is an open-boundary artefact; the discrete variance-mixing term is of the same order. The Hodge curl fraction is larger in MCSs and the signed plaquette circulation reproduces the vorticity polarity of the host flow (anticyclonic in MHWs, cyclonic in MCSs; ρ = 0.97 with ζ/f). A formal entropy production is larger in MHWs per unit mean speed. In the regression, the downstream-drift part of the upwind operator carries almost all of the skill of advection, the compression and base-exchange coefficients are strongly attenuated, and a quarter to two fifths of the mean-square target is a change of the event mean beyond any zero-sum redistribution. ### Evidence #20 [paper] Performance of the Heatwave ED Forecast Tool in the Emergency Department Network of Ticino Canton, Switzerland
Authors: Matteo Paganini, Ilaria Costantini, Ilenia Mascherona, Simone Cosci, Laura Di Domenico, Ana Maria Vicedo-Cabrera, Roberta Petrino
Venue: medRxiv
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.11.26365239
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Objectives: Heatwaves drive surges in acute care demand, yet heat-health action plans (HHAPs) lack tools to scale emergency care responses. This study validated a forecasting tool for heatwave-related emergency department (ED) demand across four clusters comprising the emergency care network of Ticino Canton, Switzerland. Methods: This retrospective, ecological, time-series study modeled daily mean temperature and ED presentations using a distributed lag non-linear model framework with autoregressive and calendar adjustment components. The model was trained on summers (June-August) 2016-2024 and tested on summer 2025, defining heatwaves as daily mean temperature >25 °C for ≥3 consecutive days. Results: A non-linear temperature-demand association was shown across the clusters, with ED visits increasing at higher temperatures. Model fit varied across clusters (R 2 0.389–0.672), and the reference model consistently outperformed naïve baselines. During two heatwaves in 2025, prediction errors remained within acceptable ranges. Baseline demand, heatwave occurrence, and model performance varied substantially within the canton. Conclusions: These findings have implications for the design of HHAPs, suggesting that emergency care preparedness should rely on locally calibrated demand forecast rather than standardized activation protocols.

### Evidence #21 [paper] Marine heat waves in vulnerable coral reef regions are skillfully predictable on multi-year timescales
Authors: Alexia Karwat, June‐Yi Lee, Christian L. E. Franzke, Yong‐Yub Kim, Eun Young Kwon, Sun‐Seon Lee
Venue: Nature Communications
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78279-1
Still open/current: yes
Problem (Mycelium's reading): The limited understanding of the predictability of marine heat waves (MHWs) in coral reef regions beyond seasonal timescales.
Method (Mycelium's reading): Analysis of Community Earth System Model version 2 simulations, ocean-assimilated experiments, and five-year hindcasts initialized annually from 1981 to 2020 to assess MHW frequency, cumulative intensity, and duration.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): 12 vulnerable coral reef regions across various ocean basins, using historical data from 1981 to 2020.
Abstract:
Marine heat waves (MHWs) increasingly threaten coral reefs, but their predictability beyond seasonal timescales remains poorly understood. Here we assess the seasonal-to-multi-year predictability of MHW frequency, cumulative intensity, and duration across 12 coral reef regions. Using Community Earth System Model version 2 simulations, ocean-assimilated experiments and five-year hindcasts initialized annually from 1981 to 2020, we show that frequency and cumulative intensity are predictable for several months to multiple years across many ocean basins, with skill varying strongly by region and season. Anthropogenic warming is the dominant source of multi-year skill across most hotspots, while internal variability limits predictability in the equatorial Pacific. El Niño-Southern Oscillation and Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation further modulate regional forecasts through coupled ocean-atmosphere processes. These findings reveal strong regional contrasts in the sources and limits of forecast skill, identifying where advance warning can support timely, targeted regional preparedness, coral reef conservation and adaptation to escalating thermal stress. The study shows that marine heatwave frequency and cumulative intensity across vulnerable coral reef regions are skillfully predictable from months to years ahead, with long-term anthropogenic warming accounting for most multi-year forecast skill, while internal variability, particularly El Niño, limits predictability in the equatorial Pacific.
