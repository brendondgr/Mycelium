Authors: Naif Sultan Alaboud
Venue: Scientific Reports
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-76473-1
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
To maximize financial benefits and sustainability throughout a project’s lifecycle, the construction industry must move beyond the partial implementation of living wall (LW). While the existing literature has focused on LW drivers, there is a lack of comprehensive evidence regarding implementation barriers. This study prioritizes and assesses the LW implementation barriers in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA) building industry. Data were collected via an expert survey and analyzed using partial least squares structural equation modeling. The model demonstrated robust internal consistency, with Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability meeting the required thresholds, and average variance extracted values exceeding the 0.50 (50%) requirement. Based on the second-order analysis, the identified LW barriers were found to be formative, but collinearity was assessed and found to be within the acceptable thresholds (VIF < 3.5), ensuring the distinct contribution of each formative indicator. The path model results showed that awareness and economic barriers ( β = 0.278), integration and complexity barriers (β = 0.303), operational and behavioral barriers (β = 0.380), and resource and structural barriers (β = 0.372) significantly impede LW implementation (all p ≤ 0.001). Conversely, maintenance and technical barriers (β = 0.097) were not significant ( p = 0.227), indicating their minimal impact compared with the other model constructs. These findings provide policy guidance concerning LW adoption in the KSA and other Gulf nations, facilitating the completion of projects by removing the barriers to LW implementation and enhancing their overall success.

Venue: Nature Communications
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77248-y
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Abstract The pathophysiology of depression involves multiple biological processes, including circuit dysfunction and impaired neuroplasticity, yet an integrative view linking these processes remains elusive. Here, we identify a convergent circuit for antidepressant response and plasticity modulation. We demonstrate that chemogenetic activation of the infralimbic cortex (IL) exerts rapid antidepressant-like effects across multiple behavioral domains in a mouse model of stress-induced depression. IL stimulation exerts top-down control over the hippocampus, enhancing structural plasticity, restoring long-term potentiation deficits and improving state-dependent network dynamics in the ventral hippocampus (vHIPP). Our findings demonstrate that the functional IL → RE→vHIPP circuit plays a central role in the antidepressant response, linking circuit activity, hippocampal plasticity, and depressive-like behaviors.

Authors: Md Shahinur Rahman, Muhammad Mainuddin Patwary, Shahreen Hasan, Asma Safia Disha, Md Pervez Kabir
Venue: Scientific Reports
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-68956-y
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Despite the growing recognition of single-use plastic (SUP) pollution as a substantial environmental challenge, there is limited understanding of the behavioral and socio-demographic factors influencing individuals’ intentions to reduce SUP usage, particularly in refugee populations. This study investigated the determinants of behavioral intentions (BI) to reduce SUP usage among the Rohingya refugee population in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, using an extended Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) model. A total of 304 respondents from 15 refugee camps were surveyed through face-to-face interviews. Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) revealed that incentive measures and perceived behavioral control showed the stronest associations with BI, followed by attitudes and environmental concern (all p < 0.001). Additionally, moral norms, past behavior, and subjective norms also showed significant but comparatively weaker associations with BI. The extended TPB model demonstrated moderate explanatory power (R² = 0.587), accounting for 58.7% of the variance in behavioral intention. These findings suggest that economic incentives and perceived behavioral control may be important factors associated with intentions to reduce plastic use, while environmental concern, moral norms, subjective norms, and past behavior were also associated with behavioral intentions. By applying the extended TPB model in a refugee context, this study contributes to the understanding of factors associated with behavioral intentions toward SUP reduction and supports the utility of this framework in humanitarian emergency settings.
