This study presents an explainable, policy-aware early warning system that leverages the most comprehensive historical record of mass mortality events (1986-2020) to forecast site-level risk in the Mediterranean coralligenous reefs. Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) are commonly defined as statistically extreme temperature events relative to a climatological baseline. While this approach provides a consistent description of ocean temperature extremes, it does not directly account for the thermal exposure experienced by marine organisms or its biological consequences. Here, we propose the Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress Wave (PMHSW) framework, which shifts the focus from temperature anomalies toward biologically relevant thermal exposure. The framework integrates temperature exceedance above a biological threshold across time and depth to estimate cumulative thermal exposure and relates this exposure to the probability of a defined biological response. The probabilistic formulation allows differences among organisms, habitats and thermal tolerances to be represented while retaining a continuous description of biological heat stress. Using synthetic examples, we demonstrate how the response function can be estimated from biological observations and how a binary event definition can be obtained when needed. The PMHSW framework is intended as a complementary approach to existing MHW definitions, particularly when the objective is to assess the potential biological consequences of marine thermal extremes under present and future climate conditions.

Abstract. This study presents cSINGV v1.0, a high-resolution (1.5 km) regional coupled atmosphere–ocean–wave modelling system developed for the Western Maritime Continent (WMC). The primary contribution of this work lies in the development of a fully coupled system enabling two-way interactions between atmosphere, ocean, and waves at kilometre scale. The model is evaluated using a six-month simulation (January–June 2019) against multiple observational and reanalysis datasets. Problem (Mycelium's reading): The intensification, increasing frequency, and duration of marine heatwave (MHW) events, particularly in vulnerable semi-enclosed coastal basins like the Adriatic Sea.
Method (Mycelium's reading): Integration of a high-resolution Regional Ocean Modeling System (ROMS) with an eddy detection and tracking algorithm and analysis of long-term observational temperature data.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): The Adriatic Sea (AS).
Abstract:
Marine heatwave (MHW) events are intensifying globally, doubling in frequency and increasing in duration and spatial distribution over recent decades. Semi-enclosed coastal basins are particularly vulnerable, and the Adriatic Sea (AS) exemplifies a fast-reacting system, making it a natural laboratory for studying climate-driven ocean variability and impacts on ecosystems, fisheries, and aquaculture. In this study, we assess the capability of a high-resolution Regional Ocean Modeling System (ROMS) to detect and simulate MHW events in the AS. We integrate this modeling framework with an eddy detection and tracking algorithm, alongside comprehensive analysis of long-term observational temperature data, to investigate the role of mesoscale eddies in modulating surface and subsurface MHW evolution. Results demonstrate that ROMS effectively reproduces the timing and spatial patterns of historical MHWs in the AS, showing strong agreement between modeled and observed MHWs, with eddy structures frequently coinciding with subsurface thermal anomalies preceding MHWs. These findings highlight the value of coupling regional ocean models with mesoscale eddy diagnostics to advance understanding of MHW occurrences and drivers, and to strengthen early-warning capabilities. Enhanced understanding of MHW precursors and eddy interactions in the AS provides a pathway for anticipating hazards, informing adaptive measures for coastal stakeholders, and improving regional climate resilience. Beyond the Adriatic, this framework offers a transferable approach to other semi-enclosed and shelf seas, where reliable detection of MHW drivers is essential. By linking detection with predictive understanding, this work contributes to broader efforts to improve preparedness for marine extremes and safeguard ecosystems and dependent economies in a warming ocean.
