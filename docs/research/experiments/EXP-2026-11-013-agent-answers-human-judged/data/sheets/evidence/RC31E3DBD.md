## Question

Is this call worth applying to for our lab, and if so, which project would it fund and who would lead it?

## Answer

Here's my honest read on this one.

## The call in brief

#1 is a Horizon Europe Mission call (HORIZON-MISS-2027-03-OCEAN-05) for **large-scale demonstration of protection, restoration and management of blue forest ecosystems** in European sea basins. €1M–€8.9M, deadline **21 September 2027**, still open. It asks for multidisciplinary projects that integrate:

- **Active or passive restoration** of marine/coastal vegetated habitats (seagrass, mangroves, salt marshes)
- **Conservation** — establishing and managing MPAs / EU Blue Parks
- **Innovative financing** — nature credits, carbon removal certification, public-private models
- **Multi-sensor research and monitoring**
- **Community engagement** and policy-maker involvement

Each proposal must target **one basin** (Atlantic/Arctic, Mediterranean, Baltic/North Sea, or Danube/Black Sea) and involve EU Member State or Associated Country agencies.

## Fit with your lab

**The strongest reason to apply:** The call explicitly asks for multi-sensor research and monitoring, and your lab's core identity is combining ocean models, satellite observations, and machine learning to forecast coastal conditions (#2). The "research and monitoring" work package is squarely in your wheelhouse. Priya Raman's satellite oceanography and coastal water quality expertise (#7) is the closest individual match in the lab, and the monitoring angle is a natural fit.

**The biggest doubt:** This is fundamentally a **restoration and management** call, not a forecasting or data-science call. The expected outcomes are about increased habitat area, improved ecosystem condition, blue carbon storage, MPA management, and novel financing mechanisms. Your lab's two projects — Marine heatwave early warning (#3) and Plastic pathways (#4) — are both about **predicting what will happen**, not about **restoring or managing ecosystems on the ground**. Neither project has a restoration, conservation, or financing component. The call also requires direct involvement of EU national/regional authorities, policy makers, and local communities in the consortium, which is a very different kind of partnership than the research collaborations your lab typically builds.

## Which project would it fund?

Honestly, **neither project as it stands**. This call doesn't map cleanly onto #3 or #4. It would require a new project or a significant reframing of one of them. The closest bridge would be:

- **#3 Marine heatwave early warning** — if you reframed it to include how heatwave forecasts inform **management decisions for blue carbon habitats** (e.g., timing of restoration interventions, MPA management responses to thermal stress). The monitoring and forecasting component fits, but you'd need to add the restoration, conservation, and financing layers.
- **#4 Plastic pathways** — a weaker fit. You could argue that debris tracking informs habitat condition assessment, but the call is about vegetated ecosystems, not litter.

## Who would lead it?

The nightly ranking names **Priya Raman** (#7) as the closest profile match (64%), followed by **Jonah Whitcombe** (#6) and **Tomás Ferreira-Lind** (#9) at 61% each. But I want to be clear about what that means: **no one in the lab has a declared profile that covers the core slots of this call** — protection, restoration, and management of blue forest ecosystems. The ranking is about research-profile proximity, not about who has the right expertise to lead a restoration and policy consortium.

In practice, the PI for this would need to be someone who can credibly lead a multidisciplinary consortium that includes EU agencies, policy makers, NGOs, and local communities, and who has a track record in marine ecosystem restoration or blue carbon. That's a different skill set from what's declared in the lab. If you were to apply, you'd likely need to **bring in a partner PI or co-PI from outside the lab** — perhaps from a marine ecology, restoration biology, or policy group — and have your lab lead the monitoring and data-science work package.

## Bottom line

This is a **partial fit**. Your lab's monitoring and forecasting expertise is a genuine asset for the research-and-monitoring component, but the call's centre of gravity is restoration, conservation, and financing — areas where the lab has no declared expertise and no existing project. It's worth applying to **only if** you can assemble a consortium that fills those gaps with partners who have the restoration and policy track record. The deadline is 21 September 2027, so you have time to build that partnership if you want to pursue it.

One thing I could not check: your institution's eligibility as an EU Member State or Associated Country entity, and whether your lab has existing relationships with EU coastal authorities. Those would be decisive for a proposal like this.

# Judging a call's fit

1. Read the call with `corpus_item` (deadlines, eligibility, amount, whether it is
   still open). If it is closed, say so first.
2. Read who it is for: `projects_get` for a project, `org_lab` for a lab,
   `me_profile` / `me_interests` for the person.
3. Compare concretely: the call's aims against the project's problem and
   method; its eligibility rules (career stage, citizenship, institution type)
   against what you know; its deadline against the project's milestones.
4. Say what fits, what does not, and what you could not check (eligibility you
   cannot see, a budget you do not know). Never guess eligibility.
5. If the question is who should apply, read the `who-should-apply` skill.

Honesty rules, as in Mylo's reports: no scores or percentages, no hype, no
"perfect fit". One plain sentence on the strongest reason and one on the
biggest doubt. Cite the call and the project by #n.
