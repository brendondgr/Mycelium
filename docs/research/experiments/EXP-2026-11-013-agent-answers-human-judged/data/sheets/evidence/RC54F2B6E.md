## Question

Does this call fit the Marine heatwave early warning project?

## Answer

**Short answer: it's a partial fit — the project's subject matter is in scope, but the call's framing is much broader and longer-term than what the project appears to be doing.**

**What fits**
- The call explicitly asks for work on "extreme events and mid-latitude weather" in the Southern Ocean, and for improving projections of near-, mid- and long-term change (#1). Marine heatwaves are exactly the kind of extreme event that fits that language.
- The call wants improved monitoring indicators and better regional models/ESMs (#1), which aligns with a project focused on early warning and forecasting.

**What does not fit (or is uncertain)**
- The call is anchored to **Antarctica and the Southern Ocean**, with a specific focus on the Antarctic continental shelves and bottom-water formation regions (#1). I could not open the project's full details (goals, milestones, region of focus) — the project record only showed its name, lab scope, and three members (#2). If the project's work is centred on mid-latitude or tropical regions (e.g. the Bay of Bengal, Pacific), the geographic fit is weak.
- The call is a **large, multi-year, multidisciplinary programme** (€5–11 M, deadline 23 Sep 2027, still open) aimed at decadal-to-millennial projections, tipping points, and ecosystem vulnerability across the whole Southern Ocean system (#1). A single early-warning project is more likely a component of such a proposal than a standalone fit.
- I could not verify eligibility details (EU coordination, consortium requirements, career stage) from what is visible here.

**Biggest doubt:** whether the project's operational region is the Southern Ocean. If it is, the call is a plausible vehicle (likely as part of a larger consortium proposal). If it is not, the fit is thin.

**What I could not check:** the project's stated goals, milestones, and geographic scope (the project detail view was not available to me), and the call's full eligibility rules. I'd suggest checking the project's own description and the Horizon call page directly before deciding.

# Judging a call's fit

1. Read the call with `corpus_item` (deadlines, eligibility, amount, whether it is
   still open). If it is closed, say so first.
2. Read who it is for: `projects_get` for a project, `org_lab` for a lab,
   `me_profile` / `me_interests` for the person.
3. Compare concretely: the call's aims against the project's problem and
   method; its eligibility rules (career stage, citizenship, institution type)
   against what you know; its deadline against the project's milestones.
4. Say what fits, what does not, and what you could not check (eligibility you
   cannot see, a budget you do not know). Never guess eligibility.
5. If the question is who should apply, read the `who-should-apply` skill.

Honesty rules, as in Mylo's reports: no scores or percentages, no hype, no
"perfect fit". One plain sentence on the strongest reason and one on the
biggest doubt. Cite the call and the project by #n.
