### Evidence #2 [paper] Alternative futures for laboratory medicine in 2050: scenarios and signposts for diagnostic capability contraction.
Authors: Yuji, Koichiro, Yuji, Wakako, Ohnishi, Hiroaki
Venue: Clinical chemistry and laboratory medicine
Year: 2026
Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42723619/
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
INTRODUCTION: Laboratory medicine has begun to describe its own future, and the images it produces are almost uniformly expansionary. What they rarely contain is a collapse scenario: a future in which the field delivers less than today. A profession planning for 2050 needs one. CONTENT: We summarise what structured foresight offers a clinical readership, entering through the threats quadrant of a strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats (SWOT) analysis, and treat the standard taxonomy of futures as a lens, not a required method. We define diagnostic capability contraction as progressive degradation rather than catastrophic disappearance, distinct from retiring tests that add nothing. Six drivers are specified: loss of laboratory-developed tests, reduced test availability, workforce attrition, supplier dependency, geographical access and payment. Evidence comes from three jurisdictions, including Japan, where one regulatory driver produced different outcomes in the same period. We set out four scenarios for 2050, one per generic image, and propose signposts as early-warning indicators, with action thresholds, for the contraction scenario. Estimates of likelihood attach to the indicators, not the futures. SUMMARY: The evidence base is uneven across drivers, and informatively so: what is not anticipated is not measured. The argument applies to our own prior work, expansionary in the same way. OUTLOOK: A set containing a contraction branch gives laboratory leadership what an expansionary set cannot: what to watch for, and when to act. A preferred future chosen without it comes from a shorter list than the field faces. Building a balanced set is work that no small group of authors can do alone.

### Evidence #20 [paper] ISO 15189:2022 in Clinical Biochemistry Laboratories: A Practical Review for Quality Improvement
Authors: Mrs. Varsha Vivek Gharat and Mr. Vivek Keshav Gharat
Venue: International Journal of Advanced Research in Science Communication and Technology
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.48175/ijarsct-38436
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Background: ISO 15189:2022 is the latest international standard that defines the requirements for quality and competence in medical laboratories. Compared with previous editions, it places greater emphasis on patient safety, risk-based thinking, impartiality, digitalization, personnel competence, and continual quality improvement. Clinical biochemistry laboratories play a vital role in patient diagnosis and treatment; therefore, effective implementation of ISO 15189:2022 is essential to ensure reliable, accurate, and timely laboratory services. Objective: This review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the requirements of ISO 15189:2022 and their practical implementation in clinical biochemistry laboratories. It highlights the major changes introduced in the 2022 edition and discusses their significance in improving laboratory quality management systems. Methods: This review is based on an analysis of ISO 15189:2022 requirements, relevant CLSI guidelines, NABL accreditation criteria, and published scientific literature related to laboratory quality management and clinical biochemistry practice. Results: The review summarizes key aspects of laboratory organization, personnel competence, equipment management, reagent control, sample management, method verification and validation, measurement uncertainty, internal quality control, external quality assessment, risk management, document control, internal audits, management review, and continual improvement. Practical examples relevant to routine clinical biochemistry laboratories are included to facilitate effective implementation.

### Evidence #21 [paper] Bridging laboratory and clinical practice: timelines and challenges in wet lab research
Authors: Nisreen Mohammed Al-Namnam, Steph Collishaw, Melanie Wheeldon
Venue: Bone and Joint Research
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1302/2046-3758.1511.bjr-2026-0221.r1
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
For surgical trainees embarking on a period of laboratory research, the transition from operating theatre to bench science can be both rewarding and disorienting. This annotation is written specifically for clinicians entering a wet laboratory environment, typically on an out-of-programme research placement, who may be unfamiliar with the realities of experimental science. Drawing on the first-hand experiences of a multidisciplinary orthopaedic research team, it explains why laboratory timelines are often longer than expected, and offers practical guidance for working effectively alongside PhD-level scientists, postdoctoral researchers, research technicians, and their trainees - collaborators whose technical expertise is indispensable to experimental success. Wet lab work involves complex protocols, tissue handling, assay optimization, and regulatory approvals, all of which can significantly extend project timelines. Effective communication, realistic planning, and mutual respect are crucial to manage unforeseen challenges and ensure that experiments are reliable, reproducible, and clinically meaningful.
