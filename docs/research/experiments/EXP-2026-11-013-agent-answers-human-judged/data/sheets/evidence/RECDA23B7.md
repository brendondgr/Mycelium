# RECDA23B7

## Question

Where does the Marine heatwave early warning project stand right now?

## Answer

Based on the provided evidence, the current status of marine heatwave early warning efforts involves the development and validation of several new, advanced systems and frameworks across different regions and timescales:

*   **Mediterranean Reefs:** A new explainable, policy-aware early-warning system has been developed to forecast site-level risk in Mediterranean coralligenous reefs using historical mass mortality data from 1986 to 2020 #1. This model has been validated on independent prospective test data from 2019 to 2020 and achieves an average precision of 0.75 #1. The system is currently available as an open-access interactive decision-support platform to support climate-adaptive Marine Spatial Planning #1.
*   **South Korean Aquaculture:** A new Hybrid GNN-BiLSTM model has been benchmarked for operational marine-heatwave early warning in South Korean aquaculture, achieving the lowest 1-hour RMSE of 0.057 °C among tested models #5. This system demonstrated a mean advisory lead time of 38 hours during the 2024 regional high water temperature alert period #5.
*   **Coral Reef Regions:** Research has confirmed that marine heatwave frequency and cumulative intensity in 12 vulnerable coral reef regions are skillfully predictable on multi-year timescales, with anthropogenic warming identified as the dominant source of this long-term forecast skill #6.
*   **Dutch Heat Warning System:** The Dutch heat early warning system has evolved from a single trigger into a tiered warning system, although a misalignment remains between the warning levels and the linked Heat-Health Action Plan #2. Current projections indicate that the frequency of heat warnings will increase substantially, rising from approximately 2 per year currently to between 3.9 and 7 by 2050, and up to 29 by 2100 #2.
*   **Subsurface and Regional Detection:** New frameworks are being established to better detect and characterize marine heatwaves, including the use of high-resolution Regional Ocean Modeling Systems (ROMS) with eddy diagnostics in the Adriatic Sea #3. Additionally, glider observations in the North Sea are being used to improve the analysis and validation of subsurface temperature anomalies, which satellite data alone cannot fully capture #10.
*   **Upwelling Collapse:** Seasonal forecasting systems have demonstrated the capability to provide early warning of upwelling collapse, such as the extreme event in the Gulf of Panama in 2025, which was accurately predicted a season in advance #14.
*   **Methodological Advances:** New probabilistic frameworks, such as the Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress Wave (PMHSW), are being proposed to shift focus from statistical temperature anomalies to biologically relevant thermal exposure #16. Furthermore, studies are evaluating whether localized interventions, such as marine cloud brightening, can effectively mitigate the impacts of predicted marine heatwaves #17.

Abstract:
Accelerating marine heatwaves are outpacing static conservation strategies, threatening the functional integrity of temperate reefs globally. Implementing “climate-smart” Marine Spatial Planning requires moving beyond retrospective impact assessment toward prospective tools that can integrate local context to anticipate vulnerability. This study presents an explainable, policy-aware early warning system that leverages the most comprehensive historical record of mass mortality events (1986-2020) to forecast site-level risk in the Mediterranean coralligenous reefs. Using a rigorous modelling framework validated on independent prospective test data (2019-2020), the model achieves high forecasting skill (average precision = 0.75, where 1.0 is perfect and the baseline is 0.70) and maintains temporal robustness by simulating real-world forecasting across the historical record. Model interpretability indicates that macroclimatic thermal stress is modulated by local factors such as depth and spatial protection. For instance, each decade since marine protected area (MPA) designation corresponds to an estimated ~48% decrease in the odds of elevated mortality (95% CI: 17–72%) under high heat stress, although this modulating signal was statistically detectable primarily in the Western Mediterranean and does not imply absolute resistance against extreme thermal anomalies. By translating these complex, context-dependent predictions into equitable, subregion-balanced alerts through an open-access interactive decision-support platform, this study provides a practical forecasting tool blueprint to optimize monitoring surveys, guide proactive triage, and support climate-adaptive Marine Spatial Planning under accelerating ocean warming.

### Evidence #2 [paper] The evolution of the Dutch heat early warning system (2007 - 2025)
Authors: Carolina Pereira Marghidan, Rob Sluijter, Justine Blanford, Hisso Homan, Peter Siegmund, Werner Hagens, Maarten van Aalst
Venue: Climate Services
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cliser.2026.100718
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Heat early warning systems (HEWS) are critical tools for anticipating hazardous heat and enabling timely response. While some of the earliest systems were developed over two decades ago, little is known about how and why systems have evolved in practice. This study therefore examines the evolution of the Dutch heat warning system from 2007 to 2025 and evaluates the future performance of current warning criteria. We analysed 18 operational documents, including seven post-event evaluations, to identify operational challenges and drivers of change. Current warning criteria were also applied to KNMI’23 climate scenarios to assess future warning frequency, duration, intensity, and season length. Results show that the warning system evolved from a single trigger for the national Heat-Health Action Plan into a tiered warning system, while the HHAP remained linked to lower warning levels, creating misalignment between warnings and actions. Criteria remained primarily climatological due to limited epidemiological evidence, and criteria changes, such as the removal of nighttime criteria, were driven primarily by operational considerations. Future projections indicate a substantial increase in heat warnings, from ~2 per year currently to 3.9–7 by 2050 and up to 29 by 2100. The findings provide lessons for meteorological and public health agencies by showing how operational, institutional, climate, and scientific factors shape the design of heat early warning systems.

Abstract. Our results highlight both the value and limitations of current reanalyses for subsurface MHW characterisation, emphasising the need for ensemble‑based approaches to quantify uncertainty.

Abstract:
Abstract Marine heatwaves (MHWs) in the Gulf of Mexico (GoM) have become more frequent and intense, yet the mechanisms governing basin‐scale events remain incompletely understood. EOF–k‐means clustering of event‐mean sea surface temperature anomalies (SSTA) during 1982–2024 identifies four recurrent, seasonally dependent spatial regimes: two stronger, longer cold‐season regimes and two weaker, shorter warm‐season regimes.
