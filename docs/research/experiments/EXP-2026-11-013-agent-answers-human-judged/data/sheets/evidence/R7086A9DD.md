## Question

What method does the Marine heatwave early warning project use?

## Answer

The Marine heatwave early warning project described in the evidence utilizes a web-based application that integrates Copernicus Marine satellite products with high-resolution regional model analysis to provide daily monitoring and 10-day forecasts #12. This system is designed to offer near real-time detection and short-term forecasting of sub-regional marine heatwaves to support marine policy and environmental management #12. The application includes a schematic dataflow and a Product User Manual, demonstrating the use of high-resolution numerical modelling for marine heatwave forecasting #12.

Setting (Mycelium's reading): Mediterranean coralligenous reefs, utilizing a historical record of mass mortality events from 1986 to 2020.
Abstract:
Accelerating marine heatwaves are outpacing static conservation strategies, threatening the functional integrity of temperate reefs globally. Implementing “climate-smart” Marine Spatial Planning requires moving beyond retrospective impact assessment toward prospective tools that can integrate local context to anticipate vulnerability. This study presents an explainable, policy-aware early warning system that leverages the most comprehensive historical record of mass mortality events (1986-2020) to forecast site-level risk in the Mediterranean coralligenous reefs. Using a rigorous modelling framework validated on independent prospective test data (2019-2020), the model achieves high forecasting skill (average precision = 0.75, where 1.0 is perfect and the baseline is 0.70) and maintains temporal robustness by simulating real-world forecasting across the historical record. Model interpretability indicates that macroclimatic thermal stress is modulated by local factors such as depth and spatial protection. For instance, each decade since marine protected area (MPA) designation corresponds to an estimated ~48% decrease in the odds of elevated mortality (95% CI: 17–72%) under high heat stress, although this modulating signal was statistically detectable primarily in the Western Mediterranean and does not imply absolute resistance against extreme thermal anomalies. By translating these complex, context-dependent predictions into equitable, subregion-balanced alerts through an open-access interactive decision-support platform, this study provides a practical forecasting tool blueprint to optimize monitoring surveys, guide proactive triage, and support climate-adaptive Marine Spatial Planning under accelerating ocean warming.

### Evidence #3 [paper] The evolution of the Dutch heat early warning system (2007 - 2025)
Authors: Carolina Pereira Marghidan, Rob Sluijter, Justine Blanford, Hisso Homan, Peter Siegmund, Werner Hagens, Maarten van Aalst
Venue: Climate Services
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cliser.2026.100718
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Heat early warning systems (HEWS) are critical tools for anticipating hazardous heat and enabling timely response. While some of the earliest systems were developed over two decades ago, little is known about how and why systems have evolved in practice. This study therefore examines the evolution of the Dutch heat warning system from 2007 to 2025 and evaluates the future performance of current warning criteria. We analysed 18 operational documents, including seven post-event evaluations, to identify operational challenges and drivers of change. Current warning criteria were also applied to KNMI’23 climate scenarios to assess future warning frequency, duration, intensity, and season length. Results show that the warning system evolved from a single trigger for the national Heat-Health Action Plan into a tiered warning system, while the HHAP remained linked to lower warning levels, creating misalignment between warnings and actions. Criteria remained primarily climatological due to limited epidemiological evidence, and criteria changes, such as the removal of nighttime criteria, were driven primarily by operational considerations. Future projections indicate a substantial increase in heat warnings, from ~2 per year currently to 3.9–7 by 2050 and up to 29 by 2100. The findings provide lessons for meteorological and public health agencies by showing how operational, institutional, climate, and scientific factors shape the design of heat early warning systems.

### Evidence #4 [paper] Rethinking Marine Heatwaves: Towards a Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress
Authors: Natalija Dunić, Krešimir Ruić, Ivica Vilibić
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.22541/essoar.15011394/v1
Still open/current: yes
Problem (Mycelium's reading): Standard marine heatwave (MHW) definitions rely on statistical temperature anomalies relative to a climatological baseline, which does not directly account for the thermal exposure experienced by marine organisms or its biological consequences.
Method (Mycelium's reading): The Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress Wave (PMHSW) framework, which integrates temperature exceedance above a biological threshold across time and depth to estimate cumulative thermal exposure and relates this exposure to the probability of a defined biological response.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): Synthetic examples used to demonstrate the estimation of response functions from biological observations and the derivation of binary event definitions.
Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) are commonly defined as statistically extreme temperature events relative to a climatological baseline. While this approach provides a consistent description of ocean temperature extremes, it does not directly account for the thermal exposure experienced by marine organisms or its biological consequences. Here, we propose the Probabilistic Marine Heat Stress Wave (PMHSW) framework, which shifts the focus from temperature anomalies toward biologically relevant thermal exposure. The framework integrates temperature exceedance above a biological threshold across time and depth to estimate cumulative thermal exposure and relates this exposure to the probability of a defined biological response. The probabilistic formulation allows differences among organisms, habitats and thermal tolerances to be represented while retaining a continuous description of biological heat stress. Using synthetic examples, we demonstrate how the response function can be estimated from biological observations and how a binary event definition can be obtained when needed. The PMHSW framework is intended as a complementary approach to existing MHW definitions, particularly when the objective is to assess the potential biological consequences of marine thermal extremes under present and future climate conditions.

Abstract:
Marine heatwave (MHW) events are intensifying globally, doubling in frequency and increasing in duration and spatial distribution over recent decades. Semi-enclosed coastal basins are particularly vulnerable, and the Adriatic Sea (AS) exemplifies a fast-reacting system, making it a natural laboratory for studying climate-driven ocean variability and impacts on ecosystems, fisheries, and aquaculture. Enhanced understanding of MHW precursors and eddy interactions in the AS provides a pathway for anticipating hazards, informing adaptive measures for coastal stakeholders, and improving regional climate resilience. Beyond the Adriatic, this framework offers a transferable approach to other semi-enclosed and shelf seas, where reliable detection of MHW drivers is essential. By linking detection with predictive understanding, this work contributes to broader efforts to improve preparedness for marine extremes and safeguard ecosystems and dependent economies in a warming ocean.

Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.31223/x5kv4t
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) negatively impact marine ecosystems and coastal economies. Here, we evaluate whether a preemptive, short-term, localized surface emission of sea salt aerosols (marine cloud brightening, MCB) can mitigate the impacts of a predicted MHW without creating significant effects beyond the intervention zone. As a case study, we apply MCB to the 2019 Northeast Pacific MHW, "The Blob 2.0," using a global climate model seasonal-to-multiyear predictability ensemble. By demonstrating local efficacy and minor remote impacts for mitigating the Blob 2.0, this work motivates further study of whether localized and temporary climate interventions can safely and effectively mitigate MHWs.

Abstract:
OCEAN-RAD P1 is a preliminary engineering study investigating radiative sky cooling as a potential means of removing heat from enclosed seawater tanks. The proposed laboratory configuration comprises 2 m² of radiating panels, a closed freshwater circulation loop and a titanium heat exchanger coupled to a 100-litre seawater tank. Initial testing is intended to take place on land, without organisms, primarily under nocturnal conditions. The study provides a thermal model, reproducible Python code, synthetic parameter studies, a proposed experimental protocol, measurement templates and an internal technical review. A second numerical implementation reproduces the steady-state results across 1,008 prescribed parameter combinations. Practical efficiency, durability, economic viability and ecological benefits remain unverified. The work is intended to support laboratory evaluation and possible future applications in small, controlled marine facilities; it does not demonstrate cooling of the open ocean or a net planetary cooling effect. AI tools were used for technical drafting, numerical implementation and internal review. The internal review used the same AI system and does not constitute independent external peer review.

### Evidence #11 [paper] Marine heatwave exposure is changing unevenly across Scottish aquaculture sites
Authors: Camila Toledo-Jaime, Jennifer A. Graham, Ben Hudson, David Andrew Moffat, Bryony L. Townhill, Steven J. Palmer
Venue: Frontiers in Marine Science
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2026.1963854
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Introduction Marine heatwaves (MHWs) are an increasing climate hazard for coastal aquaculture, yet regional assessments may not resolve how exposure varies across the locations where farms operate. Methods Here, we analysed daily ~5-km OSTIA sea surface temperature data from 1990 to 2025 at the locations of 361 active Scottish finfish and shellfish farms, represented by 218 unique grid cells, and compared satellite temperatures with in situ records from eight farms. We used principal component analysis and hierarchical clustering to describe long-term MHW exposure, and mixed-effects models to assess temporal changes. Results Two broad exposure profiles were identified. Cluster 1 was characterised by higher mean, maximum and cumulative intensity, whereas differences in event duration and frequency between clusters were smaller. Temporal trends, however, differed more strongly. MHW frequency increased at similar rates in both clusters, while event duration, cumulative intensity and severity increased more rapidly in Cluster 2. Mean event intensity changed comparatively little. Discussion These findings show that MHW exposure across Scottish aquaculture is multidimensional, spatially uneven and changing at different rates through time. The resulting hazard -exposure landscape helps bridge regional MHW assessments and individual-farm monitoring, providing a foundation for more targeted monitoring and future assessment of aquaculture vulnerability and climate risk.

### Evidence #14 [paper] Marine heat waves in vulnerable coral reef regions are skillfully predictable on multi-year timescales
Authors: Alexia Karwat, June‐Yi Lee, Christian L. E. Franzke, Yong‐Yub Kim, Eun Young Kwon, Sun‐Seon Lee
Venue: Nature Communications
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78279-1
Still open/current: yes
Problem (Mycelium's reading): The limited understanding of the predictability of marine heat waves (MHWs) in coral reef regions beyond seasonal timescales.
Method (Mycelium's reading): Analysis of Community Earth System Model version 2 simulations, ocean-assimilated experiments, and five-year hindcasts initialized annually from 1981 to 2020 to assess MHW frequency, cumulative intensity, and duration.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): 12 vulnerable coral reef regions across various ocean basins, using historical data from 1981 to 2020.
Abstract:
Marine heat waves (MHWs) increasingly threaten coral reefs, but their predictability beyond seasonal timescales remains poorly understood. Here we assess the seasonal-to-multi-year predictability of MHW frequency, cumulative intensity, and duration across 12 coral reef regions. Using Community Earth System Model version 2 simulations, ocean-assimilated experiments and five-year hindcasts initialized annually from 1981 to 2020, we show that frequency and cumulative intensity are predictable for several months to multiple years across many ocean basins, with skill varying strongly by region and season. Anthropogenic warming is the dominant source of multi-year skill across most hotspots, while internal variability limits predictability in the equatorial Pacific. El Niño-Southern Oscillation and Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation further modulate regional forecasts through coupled ocean-atmosphere processes. These findings reveal strong regional contrasts in the sources and limits of forecast skill, identifying where advance warning can support timely, targeted regional preparedness, coral reef conservation and adaptation to escalating thermal stress. The study shows that marine heatwave frequency and cumulative intensity across vulnerable coral reef regions are skillfully predictable from months to years ahead, with long-term anthropogenic warming accounting for most multi-year forecast skill, while internal variability, particularly El Niño, limits predictability in the equatorial Pacific.

Abstract:
This repository contains a collection of Python scripts for detecting, quantifying, and analyzing Marine Heatwaves (MHWs) in the Indonesian seas, with particular emphasis on their relationship with large-scale climate variability associated with the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD). Comparison of alternative climatological baselines and sensitivity assessments. 6. Analysis of MHW temporal dynamics and long-term variability. 7. Abstract:
Abstract. This study is the first of a two-part paper that summarizes the development of a prototype coastal hazard prediction system providing short-term (+48 h) forecasts of the total water level (TWL) at 50 m resolution for the province of Quebec, Eastern Canada. In this first part, the implementation of the offshore wave model component of the system, which is a regional 1 km-resolution WAVEWATCH III™(WW3) configuration for the Estuary and Gulf of St. Lawrence (EGSL), is presented and discussed. The configuration is forced by high resolution atmosphere, ocean and sea ice forecasts provided by Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) and includes a state-of-the-art parameterization of wave propagation and attenuation in sea ice that has been tuned with observations from the EGSL. Performances are assessed against wave data collected over a two-year period during which the forecasting system was running operationally, and against historical storm data using a model hindcast. Results demonstrate reasonable forecast skills both for normal and extreme wave conditions during ice-free periods with errors ranging from 15 % to 31 % of the mean wave height. However, when sea ice is present, performances are drastically reduced, primarily due to inaccuracies in the predicted ice fields at spatial scales over which wave energy typically dissipates in sea ice.

### Evidence #19 [paper] Understanding ecosystem responses to recurrent marine heatwaves
Authors: Emily Lester, Stanley Mastrantonis, Sharyn M. Hickey, Anna Lafratta, Jessica A. Benthuysen, Eben Afrifa-Yamoah, Arani Chandrapavan, martial depczynski
Venue: Biological Conservation
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2026.112016
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Marine heatwaves (MHWs) have emerged as an increasingly severe disturbance to global marine ecosystems, with documented intensifications in both frequency and magnitude over the past century. Western Australia has experienced some of the most severe MHWs on record, causing widespread ecological impacts, including coral bleaching and mortality, local species extinctions, and socioeconomic impacts such as fishery closures, making the region a critical case study of climate-driven change in the marine domain. Most recently, a severe MHW during summer 2024/2025 has caused widespread disruption to marine life, reinforcing the region's vulnerability to repeated extreme events. While extensive research has documented the impacts of these events, most studies have examined individual heatwaves or specific taxonomic groups in isolation, limiting our understanding of cumulative and cascading effects across the ecosystem. Here, we combine a scoping review of published and emerging evidence with oceanographic analyses to assess the cumulative ecological impacts of MHWs across Western Australia. Using iconic MHWs as case studies, we reveal widespread ecological consequences, spanning habitat degradation, mass invertebrate mortality events, food web disruption, and spatial redistribution of fisheries' indicator species. Accordingly, we propose adaptive management strategies for future MHWs. These strategies include the relocation of vulnerable species, implementation of adaptive fisheries management frameworks, and development of rapid response monitoring programs targeting key benthic indicator groups. We emphasise that the successful implementation of such strategies hinges upon the accurate and timely prediction of future MHW events.

### Evidence #20 [paper] Operational Validation of a Deterministic Weather-Window Model for Offshore Operations in the Southern Baltic Sea
Authors: Maria Kubacka, Juliusz B. Gajewski
Venue: Water
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.3390/w18182337

Problem (Mycelium's reading): Reliable assessment of weather accessibility for offshore operations in fetch-limited and highly variable wave conditions.
Method (Mycelium's reading): Validation of a deterministic threshold model using 82 years of hourly ERA5 reanalysis data against observed weather standby hours from project records, applying significant wave height thresholds (Hs ≤ 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 m) and a 24 h weather-window criterion.
Setting (Mycelium's reading): Three survey vessels operating in the Polish Exclusive Economic Zone between 2022 and 2026.
Abstract:
Reliable assessment of weather accessibility is essential for offshore operations, particularly in areas such as the southern Baltic Sea, characterized by fetch-limited and highly variable wave conditions. This study combines a methodological review of weather-window approaches with operational validation of deterministic threshold methods. The review identified seven methodological groups: deterministic threshold, probabilistic, ensemble, hindcast-based, stochastic, machine learning, and hybrid approaches. Probabilistic, ensemble, and hybrid approaches offer greater potential for short-term forecasting, whereas deterministic methods were considered suitable for long-term strategic planning. This approach was therefore selected for validation against operational survey records. The framework uses 82 years of hourly ERA5 reanalysis data, significant wave height thresholds (Hs ≤ 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 m), and a 24 h weather-window criterion. Model-derived monthly downtime was compared with observed weather standby hours documented in project records for 40 monthly observations from three survey vessels operating in the Polish Exclusive Economic Zone between 2022 and 2026. The model overestimated monthly downtime, with a mean bias error (MBE) of +73.7 h, MAE = 108.4 h, and RMSE = 135.9 h. The 95% limits of agreement ranged from −152.9 to +300.4 h, with r = 0.764 and R2 = 0.584. The results indicate that the framework is more suitable for long-term strategic assessment of weather accessibility than for precise prediction of downtime during individual survey campaigns.
