## Question

Which paper in our feed matters most for the Plastic pathways project, and why?

## Answer

The clear standout is **#3 — "A tide-resolving beaching kernel for marine litter: development and application to field records from Uido, Republic of Korea"** (Lim & Hong, 2026, Zenodo).

Why it matters most for Plastic pathways:

- **It is the only item on the slate that is actually about marine litter.** The rest are ocean forecasting (#4), computer vision (#5, #6), remote sensing of lakes and wetlands (#7–#11), and marine cloud dynamics (#12) — none of which touch debris pathways.
- **It is directly methodological, not just topical.** It ships a three-phase *beaching kernel* plus six other particle kernels for OceanParcels 3.1.4, with the scripts that ran the particle-tracking experiments and the aggregated results behind every number in the manuscript. That is a ready-made component for modeling how floating debris moves and where it strands — the core question of a pathways project.
- **It is validated against field records** (beach surveys at Uido, Korea, using ROMS flow fields), so the kernel is tested against real observations rather than being purely theoretical.
- **It is reproducible and open** — code, scripts, results, and a verification manifest are all in the deposit, with the large trajectory and flow-field files available on request.

One caveat: the project's confirmed state (goals, method, blockers) isn't visible to me right now, so I'm judging fit against the project's topic and the lab's interests rather than a specific open blocker. If the team has a particular blocker — say, choosing a stranding model or validating against local beach data — #3 is the item to pull first.

Suggested next step: have Jonah or Priya read the README and kernel description in #3 to see whether the OceanParcels setup maps onto your own flow fields, and request the trajectory/flow-field files if you want to benchmark against their Uido case.
