Abstract:
This data package contains spatiotemporal snow water equivalent (SWE) data generated by a hybrid machine-learning snow model based on a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network. The HySWE dataset covers the period from 1 October 1980 to 30 September 2020 at daily resolution, and extends to all northern hemisphere pixels containing a seasonal snowpack at 30 arcsecond resolution (approximately 1 km). The hybrid model was trained using in situ snow measurements from stations across the Northern Hemisphere, taking as predictors high-resolution meteorological and topographical data, as well as ERA5-Land SWE. The trained model was subsequently used to reconstruct daily SWE, producing the dataset provided in this package. Owing to the dataset's high spatial resolution and long temporal coverage, it is suitable for global and regional snow and hydrological studies, as well as climate change analysis.

Abstract:
Accurate forecasting of tax revenues is essential for regional fiscal planning, budget management, and effective allocation of public financial resources. This study investigates the applicability of machine learning and time-series forecasting approaches to regional periodic tax revenue forecasting using monthly tax revenue data from Syrdarya Region, Uzbekistan. The dataset contains observations for 12 major tax categories covering the period from January 2023 to December 2025. Two forecasting approaches were evaluated: Ridge Regression with temporal trend and seasonal sine-cosine features, and the ARIMA(1,1,1) time-series model. To preserve the chronological structure of the data, observations from 2023–2024 were used for model training, January–June 2025 for validation, and July–December 2025 for testing. The models were evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE). On the test set, Ridge Regression achieved an MAE of 4038.43, RMSE of 4860.45, and MAPE of 32.10%, while ARIMA(1,1,1) obtained an MAE of 4030.45, RMSE of 6082.61, and MAPE of 38.55%. Although ARIMA demonstrated a slightly lower MAE, Ridge Regression provided lower RMSE and MAPE, indicating better overall forecasting performance. The results also show that model performance varies considerably across individual tax categories, suggesting that the characteristics of each revenue series should be considered when selecting a forecasting method. The study demonstrates the potential of machine learning-based forecasting for supporting regional tax revenue planning and provides a basis for developing more advanced hybrid forecasting systems. The primary out-of-sample evaluation was conducted over a six-month forecasting horizon from July to December 2025, while the final prospective forecasting exercise covered a 12-month horizon from January to December 2026.

This dataset provides hourly surface wind records from 961 meteorological stations across Brazil, combining original observations with records reconstructed using a physically constrained machine-learning framework. The reconstruction approach integrates temporal and spatial information with pressure-derived predictors, while preserving all available original wind observations. Temporal coverage varies by station based on its operational history; therefore, not all stations cover the same period. The associated study focuses on data from 1980 to 2024. The reconstructed component is intended primarily for gap filling and improvement of temporal continuity. Validation results reported in the associated peer-reviewed study indicate that reconstructed values may underestimate wind-speed climatology and the upper tail of the distribution. Reconstructed records should therefore not be interpreted as direct observations or as exact representations of extreme wind conditions. Venue: Theoretical and Applied Climatology
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00704-026-06521-3

Abstract:
High-resolution, long-term near-surface air temperature ( T a ) data are fundamental for regional climate monitoring and extreme temperature hazard assessment, particularly in topographically complex regions with sparse observations. However, achieving both temporal continuity and spatial detail in daily T a fields remains challenging under heterogeneous landscapes. This study develops a spatial-background residual framework to generate a 1 km resolution daily T a dataset (ZJ-DAT) for Zhejiang Province, China, spanning 1961–2020, covering daily minimum, mean and maximum temperatures. The framework integrates a high-resolution climatological baseline derived from hourly 1 km gridded temperatures for 2008–2018 with spatially interpolated daily anomalies from meteorological stations. Three interpolation schemes are evaluated for comparison: inverse distance weighting (IDW), lapse-rate–adjusted IDW (IDW + LR), and multiple linear regression (MLR); cross-validation results indicate that inverse distance weighting without lapse-rate correction provides the most robust overall performance. Further analysis reveals strong spatiotemporal non-stationarity in near-surface temperature lapse rates, which approach zero or become negative in winter lowlands but exceeding 7.0 °C km⁻¹ in summer mountainous regions, indicating that uniform lapse rate can introduce systematic elevation-related biases over complex terrain. ZJ-DAT shows high agreement with station observations and reanalysis products, demonstrating its reliability for climate applications. Based on this dataset, extreme temperature characteristics and associated hazards are further examined. Results show that ZJ-DAT provides spatially coherent and temporally consistent estimates of temperature extremes, enabling improved assessment of extreme temperature hazards across different regions and return periods. The proposed framework is transferable to other topographically complex, data-limited observations and provides a reliable basis for high-resolution climate monitoring, hazard assessment, and adaptation planning.
