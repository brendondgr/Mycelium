## Answer

There are two conferences in the Plastic pathways slate, and one of them fits well:

**#6 — 47th IEEE International Geoscience and Remote Sensing Symposium**
- Still open, with an abstract deadline of 2027-01-11 and the event in July 2027.
- This is the better fit. The project's slate is full of ocean-observation work — particle tracking with ROMS flow fields (#4), satellite imagery analysis (#8) — and IGARSS is the main venue for exactly that kind of remote-sensing and ocean-observation research. A paper on debris pathways observed or modeled from satellite data would be right at home there.

**#11 — IEEE International Conference on Sensing, Communication, and Networking**
- Still open, manuscript deadline 2026-12-01 (so about three weeks away).
- This one is a weaker fit. It's a communications and networking conference, so it would only suit the project if the work had a strong sensor-network or data-communication angle, which the current slate doesn't show.

One honest caveat: I can see the titles, deadlines and the project's slate, but I don't have the full scope notes for either conference, so I can't rule out specific track requirements. # Judging a call's fit

1. Read the call with `corpus_item` (deadlines, eligibility, amount, whether it is
   still open). If it is closed, say so first.
2. Read who it is for: `projects_get` for a project, `org_lab` for a lab,
   `me_profile` / `me_interests` for the person.
3. Compare concretely: the call's aims against the project's problem and
   method; its eligibility rules (career stage, citizenship, institution type)
   against what you know; its deadline against the project's milestones.
4. Say what fits, what does not, and what you could not check (eligibility you
   cannot see, a budget you do not know). Never guess eligibility.
5. If the question is who should apply, read the `who-should-apply` skill.

Honesty rules, as in Mylo's reports: no scores or percentages, no hype, no
"perfect fit". One plain sentence on the strongest reason and one on the
biggest doubt. Cite the call and the project by #n.
