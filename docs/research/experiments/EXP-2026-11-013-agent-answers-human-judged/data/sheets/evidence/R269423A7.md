The evidence contains several conferences and workshops, but they do not match the specific description of a "Plastic pathways" project:
*   Evidence #10 describes the "2026 ACCESS Community Workshop on Land and Coupled Modelling," which focused on connecting machine learning to earth system science #10.

### Evidence #3 [paper] Breaking barriers: advancing strategies to foster a circular economy through additive manufacturing – a case study in the plastics industry
Authors: Alexander Bade, Rebecca Kißner, Jacob Lohmer, Rainer Lasch
Venue: Journal of Business Economics
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11573-026-01283-w
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Abstract The manufacturing industry faces the challenge of using resources more efficiently and implementing circular practices. A promising pathway is to establish a circular economy (CE) through additive manufacturing (AM), enabling on-demand production with low material input and allowing the use of recycled materials. Despite this potential, practical barriers hinder its adoption and are insufficiently understood from a multi-stakeholder perspective. This paper combines stakeholder theory with the circular supply chain management concept in a qualitative multiple-case study to examine the perspectives and interactions of the actors involved. The findings separate the general technical adoption barriers of AM from the socioeconomic and relational barriers of circularity, which arise from power asymmetries, information asymmetries, and coordination failures among interdependent actors of differing salience. These barriers vary substantially with firms’ initial conditions, including their AM technology maturity, their engagement in closed-loop practices, and the managerial relevance they assign to circularity. The study develops five propositions based on the cross-case analysis and identifies three distinct pathways from barrier to solution for firms at different stages of circular supply chain development. Trust-based partnerships between manufacturing and recycling companies can close material loops locally, but they remain fragile amid price competition from virgin materials. Durable circularity therefore requires governance safeguards, including guaranteed-offtake contracts, verifiable production information, and consolidated reverse logistics, all supported by regulatory intervention. The study contributes to the CE and operations management literature by providing a deeper understanding of these barriers and proposing actionable measures for stakeholders to promote circularity in the specific AM domain.

Abstract:
Abstract. Building an academic career requires a high level of dedication to research, frequent publishing, and maintaining visibility within the academic community. Scientific conferences are the central platforms for networking and knowledge exchange, making them keystones of an academic career. However, the early stages of an academic career often coincide with family planning considerations. Many young researchers face the challenge of balancing professional responsibilities with family obligations. A balancing act that becomes particularly difficult during non-routine work events such as conferences. Such occasions tend to pose significant organizational challenges for families. As mothers still provide most of the care, they are disproportionately affected. Our findings are distilled into guidelines for conference organizers, allowing them to better address the work-family conflict and transform their conferences towards more equity, diversity, and inclusion.

### Evidence #9 [paper] Selecting Materials Outreach Topics from Community Repair Demand: Organizational Weighting, Temporal Transfer and Local Fit
Authors: Chahn Yong Jung
Venue: Journal of Materials Education (Electronic)
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.71448/jme2026346
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
Community repair events bring materials science into contact with familiar products, but outreach teams must decide which objects deserve limited preparation time. This study evaluates topic selection under two representation rules: equal influence for each recorded repair attempt and equal influence for each repair group. The Open Repair Alliance collection contains 48,669 attempts. Selection used 17,805 attempts from 95 groups, followed by evaluation on 18,802 attempts from 126 groups. The four topics selected by attempt weighting were laptops, lamps, separate audio equipment and coffee makers. Group weighting replaced the latter two with mobile phones and small kitchen appliances. Its selection-period advantage in average group coverage contracted from 3.09 to 0.43 percentage points in the following year. Network-stratified group resampling gave a 95% interval of −1.29 to 2.28 points for that contrast, while attempt coverage decreased by 3.27 points. Local differences had opposing signs: 58 groups gained coverage, 63 lost coverage and five were unchanged. Resampling the selection groups recovered the complete topic sets in only 19.28% and 35.84% of draws. Increasing the kit to eight topics made the two rules select identical categories; ten topics were needed to exceed one-half of average group demand. These results establish the consequences of representation choices without attributing learning gains to category matching. A shared materials kit therefore requires an explicit intended beneficiary, host-specific preparation and an educational assessment independent of repair completion.

Abstract:
This systematic integrative review examines how collaboration between education providers and workplace institutions is constructed in the preparation and professional development of early childhood care and education educators. Guided by activity theory and the concepts of boundary crossing and boundary objects, the review synthesizes 28 English- and Chinese-language sources published between 2023 and 2026. Searches were conducted in ERIC, OpenAlex, and CNKI, supplemented by citation and manual searching. The thematic synthesis identified two interrelated patterns. First, collaboration was frequently constrained by tensions between institutional rules and mediating tools, including curriculum frameworks, practicum handbooks, assessment criteria, workplace routines, and performance requirements. The findings offer practical implications for vocational colleges, early childhood institutions, teacher educators, workplace mentors, and policymakers. Further longitudinal research is required to test and refine the proposed pathway across different institutional and national contexts.

### Evidence #19 [paper] Volumetric Waste: Containing Polymorphous Plastic in Bangkok’s Circular Economies
Authors: Olivia Meyer
Venue: Geoforum
Year: 2026
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoforum.2026.104858
Still open/current: yes
Abstract:
This paper examines how volumetric reasoning permeates everyday practices in Bangkok’s circular economies. While circular economies calculate, value, and contain plastic waste, mobile waste flows are hidden from view and often evade regulation. As a result, circular economy initiatives struggle to account for low-value waste, microplastics, and chemicals that proliferate through land, water, air, and blood in bodies. In these instances, waste travels and transforms volumetrically without a traceable latitude and longitude. Similarly, the actual dimensions of plastic objects impact their ability to be valued in the circular economy. Drawing on seventeen months of ethnographic fieldwork in Bangkok, Thailand, this paper documents how circular economy practitioners manage waste that takes on new materialities as it moves through plastic circularity loops. Nonstate waste practitioners invoke volumetric reasoning as they grapple with circular economy solutions (e.g., recovering, repurposing, and upcycling plastics). My argument is twofold: first, I argue that practitioners’ everyday volumetric practices expose the limits of formal circular economy policy. Second, I argue that volumetric reasoning gives practitioners a set of techniques for reclaiming agency over the everyday management of waste. By attending to commonplace yet novel spatial dimensions of plastic circularity, this paper contributes to emerging scholarship at the intersection of political ecology and volumetric analyses of plastic pollution in Asia.
