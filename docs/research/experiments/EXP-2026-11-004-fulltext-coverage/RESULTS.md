# EXP-2026-11-004 — Results

## 1. Headline

The full-text tool retrieved the full text of 108 of 200 sampled corpus papers
(0.54) from free sources alone, and 108 of the 169 it could identify (0.639).
The pre-registered hypothesis (0.25–0.45, PMC the largest source) is
**falsified on both counts**: coverage is higher, and OpenAlex's open-access
locations served more papers (0.30) than PubMed Central (0.235).

## 4. Interpretation

- **The tool is useful without any publisher keys.** More than half of a random
  draw from the live corpus came back as full text, median body 38,905
  characters, with no key the operator has to obtain. Elsevier, Springer,
  Wiley and Unpaywall were all off.
- **OpenAlex is the workhorse, not PMC.** Mycelium's corpus is broader than the
  biomedical one the engine was built on, and OpenAlex's open-access locations
  (repositories, publisher OA pages, PDFs) cover much of it. arXiv served one
  paper: arXiv ids are rare in this corpus (223 of 35,209), and arXiv papers
  that also have a journal DOI resolve to it.
- **The 28 `identifier_mismatch` stops are the corpus's error, not the
  tool's.** Every one was a record whose stored identifiers belong to two
  different papers (for example PMID 42389617, a 2026 paper, stored with the
  DOI of a 2004 *Brain and Language* article). The tool refused to fetch rather
  than return the wrong paper, which is the behaviour the design asks for. The
  follow-up check (`check_ids.py`) found that 17 of the 92 sampled papers with
  a PMID (0.185) carry a DOI, and 26 (0.283) a PMCID, that is not the paper's
  own — all PubMed-sourced. The cause is the PubMed scraper
  (`tools/retrieval/sources/pubmed.py`) reading `.//ArticleIdList/ArticleId`,
  which includes the ids of every cited reference, with the last one winning.
  That is why the PMCID-bearing papers retrieved at only 0.64: 27 of the 75
  had a PMCID that was not theirs.
- Fixing the scraper (and repairing the stored rows) would turn most of those
  28 stops into fetches — 27 had a PMC deposit on paper — so the rate on a
  clean corpus should be higher still. That is a prediction, not a
  measurement (Q-057).

## 5. Threats to validity

- **One sample, one day, one network.** 200 papers from a live database that
  was not snapshotted; provider coverage and rate limits change. The sample
  ids are frozen in `data/sample.json`, so the draw can be re-run.
- **"Full text" is the engine's own test** (a converted body of at least 500
  characters, 5,000 for HTML). No person checked that each body is the right
  paper's complete text. An HTML page or a PDF that is the paper's
  supplementary file could pass the length test.
- **The configuration is this instance's.** No contact email, so Unpaywall
  was off; another instance with one set, or with publisher keys on a
  subscribing network, would score differently.
- The denominator includes 3 papers with no identifier at all and 28 the tool
  refused because of corpus errors; both rates (all 200, and the 169 it could
  identify) are reported for that reason.

## 6. What surprised us

That the largest effect on coverage in the live corpus is a data-quality bug
upstream of this tool: about one in five PubMed-sourced papers carries a DOI
that belongs to a paper it cites.
