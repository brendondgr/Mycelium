# Venue Tracks: What Each Venue Accepts, and Which Mycelium Papers Fit

As of 2026-10-24, from each venue's official pages (see Sources). A venue
appears here only if a paper that serves Mycelium's direction can go there; the
venues that fail that test are listed at the end, with the reason.

Dates for editions not yet announced are the previous edition's, marked
**prev.** Ranks are from ICORE2026. Any accepted paper normally needs an author
registered and presenting, so a trip before the grant starts needs other money.

**The direction every idea must serve:**

- An open-source research assistant that any lab can host on its own hardware,
  down to a laptop.
- Recommendations from open sources (papers, grants, fellowships, conferences),
  with every deadline and update tracked.
- Interest profiles, and AI-written daily or weekly briefings.
- Small models: BM25, compact embeddings, decision models, a small
  mixture-of-experts language model.
- Trust through deterministic code: citations generated by code, and profile
  changes proposed for approval.
- A platform of open tools, starting with a human-AI collaborative hypothesis
  engine.

The committed submissions are in [`publication-plan.md`](publication-plan.md):
JOSS, US-RSE'27, IUI 2028 and the SIGIR 2027 demo. This file covers everything
else that fits.

## Research-software venues

### JOSS (journal; CORE does not rank journals)

| Type | Length | Review | Archival | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Software paper | 750–1,750 words | Two or more reviewers, openly on GitHub | Crossref DOI; the repository is archived on Zenodo or figshare | Rolling, once the repository has been public for six months |

Required sections: Summary, Statement of need, State of the field, Software
design, Research impact statement, AI-usage disclosure, then acknowledgements
and references. Since January 2026, JOSS judges research impact, intellectual
contribution and open-source practice rather than developer time, wants the
human work shown, and keeps short-lived, single-use code out of scope.

- **Mycelium's core library** (recommendation, extraction and briefing tools).
  This is the committed submission.
- **Later, a released tool as its own package.** The hypothesis engine, for
  example, once it is mature and used outside its own study.

### US-RSE'27 (Ann Arbor, October 25–27, 2027; not ranked)

Tentative: submissions due **mid-April 2027**, posters late June.

| Type | Length | Published |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | Up to 10 pp, IEEE template | Zenodo; papers and talks may be extended into a Future Generation Computer Systems special issue (40% new content) |
| Talk | 1-page abstract | Abstract and slides on Zenodo |
| Notebook | 300-word abstract plus a public repository | Zenodo |
| Poster | 1-page abstract | Zenodo |
| Birds-of-a-feather session (75–90 min), workshop (120 min), tutorial (new) | Description plus a 1-page abstract | Not in the proceedings |

All types are peer-reviewed and need a short statement of fit with US-RSE.

- **Paper: the experience paper.** Committed.
- **Notebook: run Mycelium's evaluation on your own laptop.** A public, rerunnable
  evaluation, which is exactly "evaluation anyone can rerun".
- **Birds-of-a-feather session: self-hosted, open AI research tools for labs.**
  For recruiting contributors and pilot labs.
- **Tutorial: deploy an open research assistant for your lab.** Using the install
  run by an AI agent.
- **The FGCS special issue** turns the experience paper into a journal article.

### PEARC27 (Raleigh, July 11–15, 2027; not ranked; ACM proceedings)

PEARC26 rules, the most recent:

| Type | Length | Published | Deadline (prev.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full paper (Applications, Systems, Workforce, or research-software-engineering track) | Up to 12 pp | ACM proceedings | Feb 9 |

| Talk-only abstract, panel, birds-of-a-feather session, tutorial | 1–4 pp | Not in the proceedings | Feb–Mar |

Review is single-blind, so naming Mycelium is fine. The research-software track
asks for practice and lessons rather than a product pitch.

- **Short paper or poster: one system, three tiers.** Run times and quality on a
  laptop, a lab server and a university cluster. Its audience runs those
  clusters.
- **An alternative home for the experience paper.** It would be in the ACM
  Digital Library and earlier than US-RSE. US-RSE papers must be original,
  though, so the same paper cannot go to both.

### ACM REP '27 (Salt Lake City, July 12–15, 2027; not ranked; ACM DL)

REP '26 rules: research, survey, vision or experience papers, 10 pp full or 4 pp
short. Double-blind review. Artifacts are mandatory for full papers with
results. Abstracts are due around mid-March (prev.).

- **Experience paper: making an AI research assistant's evaluation rerunnable.**
  The research record (protocols written before running, manifests, figures
  generated from data) applied to a working system across hardware tiers.

### SciPy 2027 (not announced; not ranked)

SciPy 2026 offered talks, posters, tutorials (at least half hands-on), and an
optional proceedings paper (at most 6,000 words, open review, CC-BY with a DOI,
no fee). Open review, proposals around February–March (prev.).

  laptop.**
- **Talk in the maintainers and community track: running a lab tool as an open
  project.** Optional.

### ACM CSCW (rank A; rolling; 2027 in Philadelphia, October 16–20)

There are no deadlines. Papers are accepted as journal articles in *PACM HCI*
(or *TOCHI*), and acceptance earns a presentation slot. Review tracks are
Design/Theory, Mixed Methods, Qualitative, Quantitative and Systems. The Systems
track allows minimal evaluation when the technical novelty is high. Previous
cycles reviewed anonymously and asked authors not to publicize the work during
review.

- **A field study: how a department and its labs use shared briefings and
  project reports.** Lab-level collaboration, which is Mycelium's core.
- **The fallback for the hypothesis engine study.** With no deadline, it absorbs
  any slip past IUI.

## Anonymity, in one rule

The repository will be public under the name Mycelium. **Tracks that are not
anonymous** (resource, demo, posters and demos, doctoral consortia, US-RSE,
PEARC, JOSS) can link it openly. **Double-anonymous tracks** need the system
renamed, an anonymized code link (anonymous.4open.science), and the team's own
papers cited in the third person. RecSys and CHIIR also require any
non-anonymous preprint to have a different title and abstract, and forbid
posting one during review.
